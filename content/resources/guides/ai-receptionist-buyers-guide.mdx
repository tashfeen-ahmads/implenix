# The Implenix AI Receptionist Buyer's Guide

This guide is for the operator or ops lead evaluating AI receptionists for the first time. It gives you a working framework to pick the right vendor and tier, plus the specific questions to ask before signing.

## Step 1, Determine your tier

The AI receptionist market has settled into three tiers. Picking the wrong tier is the most common expensive mistake.

**Tier 1, DIY templated ($50 to $200 per month).** Fits solo operators, single-location shops handling under 200 calls per month with straightforward booking. Fast to deploy (an afternoon), limited customization.

**Tier 2, Platform plus agency ($500 to $1,500 per month plus $2,000 to $8,000 setup).** Fits multi-location small businesses, 200 to 1,000 calls per month, real integration needs (ServiceTitan, Clio, Boulevard). Discovery week required.

**Tier 3, Fully custom ($1,000 to $2,500 plus per month, $10,000 to $50,000 setup).** Fits multi-location chains, regulated verticals, or operations with unique compliance or integration needs.

Match your tier to your operation. Do not buy up.

## Step 2, Score your operation

Answer these five questions honestly to confirm the tier:

1. Monthly inbound call volume: under 200, 200 to 1,000, or 1,000 plus?
2. Software integrations needed: Google Calendar plus basic CRM, or complex practice management systems?
3. Call variability: single intent per call, or multiple intents with judgment routing?
4. Compliance requirements: none, or HIPAA / TCPA / licensed industry?
5. Internal tuning ownership: all of it, some of it, or none of it?

Higher scores on any question push you toward higher tiers.

## Step 3, Do the ROI math

Use the 4-variable formula:

Monthly calls times miss rate times close rate on answered times average client value equals current monthly missed-call cost.

Multiply that ceiling by 0.7 for realistic recovery. Compare against tier cost.

For a business doing 800 calls per month at 30% miss rate, 55% close rate, and $340 average job value: current loss is 44,880 dollars per month. Realistic recovery at Tier 2 pricing (1,400 dollars per month) is 31,416 dollars per month.

If the math produces less than 2.5x monthly ROI, the tier is wrong or the deployment is not for your operation.

## Step 4, Evaluate vendors within your tier

Vendors within a tier vary meaningfully. Test the following:

**Voice quality on real calls.** Call the vendor's demo number three times: standard booking, interrupt the agent mid-sentence, ask an out-of-scope question. Listen for natural pacing, graceful interrupt handling, clean decline.

**Response latency.** Time from you stopping speaking to agent starting. Under 500ms feels natural. Over 900ms feels slow.

**Integration surface.** Ask for a specific list of every integration native vs webhook vs not supported. Verify your critical systems.

**Escalation logic.** Ask to speak with a human. Get frustrated. Ask compound questions. Watch how routing decisions happen.

**Recording, transcript, and QA workflow.** Where recordings live, retention policy, access controls, how you review and score AI-handled calls.

**Reference customers in your industry.** Two reference calls minimum. The single highest-signal thing you can do before signing.

## Step 5, Review the compliance layer

Whether or not you are in a regulated industry, baseline compliance:

- Recording notification in the greeting.
- TCPA compliance on any outbound calls.
- Access-controlled storage for recordings and transcripts.
- Written breach protocol.

For HIPAA-covered deployments, add:
- Signed BAA across the vendor stack.
- Encrypted at rest and in transit.
- Minimum-necessary intake.

For legal deployments, add:
- Ethics hard-stop list.
- Conflict-check integration.
- Attorney-client confidentiality handling.

For financial services, add:
- Licensing state gates.
- No-rate-quote scope enforcement.
- CCO / compliance officer review of greeting language.

## Step 6, Review pricing structure

Get an itemized quote:
- Build fee (one-time).
- Monthly platform.
- Per-minute or per-call rate (if applicable).
- Integrations included vs extra.
- Tuning retainer (monthly).
- Support level.

Anyone giving you a single number is hiding the breakdown. Fixed monthly pricing beats per-minute in most cases.

## Step 7, Pilot before committing

Do not sign a long-term contract without at least a 30-day production pilot. Reputable vendors offer this. Non-reputable vendors do not.

## Red flags to walk away from

- Unlimited minutes for under 300 dollars per month.
- Setup fee under 500 dollars for anything above Tier 1.
- Multi-year contract required for a first-time deployment.
- No specifics on integration approach for your specific software.
- Voice demo that sounds robotic with vendor claim that this is normal.
- No clear escalation-to-human path.

## What comes after go-live

The deployment is not finished at cutover. Ongoing:

- Monthly QA review of 20 to 30 flagged calls.
- Prompt tuning based on findings.
- Performance reporting.
- Quarterly deep-review.

Deployments that get monitored aggressively in months 1 to 3 have significantly better long-term performance than deployments that ship and are left alone.
