# The 90-Day AI Receptionist Deployment Playbook

Every AI receptionist deployment follows the same 90-day arc. This playbook covers the operator's side of that arc, week by week, with the tasks, KPIs, and calibration points that determine whether the deployment stays healthy or drifts.

## First 72 hours, Discovery through go-live

Covered in depth in the implementation guide. Quick recap:

- Hours 0 to 24: kickoff and discovery call, recording review, script draft.
- Hours 24 to 48: script iteration, integration wiring.
- Hours 48 to 72: test-call battery, your sign-off, go-live.

**Your side tasks:**
- Attend kickoff and discovery calls.
- Provide call recordings and CRM access.
- Review and approve script drafts the same day.
- Run test calls during the client test window.

## Weeks 1 to 3, Silent-monitoring and initial tuning

The agent is live. First-week production behavior sets the tone.

**What we do:**
- Silent-monitor 100 percent of calls first 48 hours.
- Review flagged calls daily.
- Push minor prompt adjustments as needed.
- Weekly performance report to you.

**Your side tasks:**
- Listen to 10 to 20 calls yourself. Not to catch problems, to build intuition for how the agent is handling.
- Notify us of any callers who complained or reported issues.
- Verify CRM records are landing correctly.
- Confirm bookings are appearing on the right calendars.

**KPIs to watch:**
- Answer rate (should be 95 percent plus).
- Capture rate (calls with defined outcome / total answered).
- Transfer rate (percentage transferring to humans).
- Booking count vs prior period.

## Week 4, First monthly review

End of month 1. First full performance report.

**What we cover in the 30-minute review:**
- Total call volume and capture rate.
- Transfer patterns by reason.
- Any escalation issues or complaints.
- 5 to 10 flagged calls for joint listening.
- Prompt tuning proposals.
- Any scope adjustments you want.

**Your side tasks:**
- Attend the review.
- Approve prompt tuning changes.
- Flag any operational shifts in your business we should reflect.

## Weeks 5 to 8, Steady-state ramp

Deployment is in production; tuning is incremental.

**What we do:**
- Weekly QA on flagged calls (5 to 10 per week).
- Push tuning adjustments through canary.
- Track KPI trends.

**Your side tasks:**
- Continue listening to a sample of calls (5 to 10 per week is enough after week 4).
- Report any patterns you notice.
- Verify any downstream workflows are firing correctly.

**KPIs to watch:**
- Capture rate should trend up 3 to 8 points from week 3.
- Transfer rate should stabilize.
- Booking-to-close conversion (if you track it) should hold.

## Week 8, Second monthly review

**Focus for month 2:**
- Trend analysis on all KPIs.
- Deployment health assessment.
- Any scope expansion opportunities identified.
- Detailed review of 10 to 15 flagged calls.
- Team feedback: how is your staff experiencing the deployment.

## Weeks 9 to 12, Optimization and scope evaluation

Deployment is stable. Focus shifts to optimization.

**What we do:**
- Continue weekly QA.
- Identify patterns for prompt improvement.
- Assess whether additional integrations or features would benefit.

**Your side tasks:**
- Evaluate whether the deployment is delivering the ROI you projected.
- Consider scope expansion (outbound sequences, additional integrations, additional locations).
- Provide feedback for the quarterly review.

## Week 12, First quarterly review

End of month 3. Comprehensive deployment health review.

**What we cover in the 60-minute review:**
- 90-day KPI report vs baseline.
- Actual ROI vs projected ROI.
- Deployment strengths and weaknesses.
- Recommended next-quarter improvements.
- Scope expansion discussion.
- Contract renewal or continuation discussion.

**Your side tasks:**
- Attend with any stakeholders (finance if ROI decisions involved).
- Bring specific questions and requests.
- Provide honest feedback on the vendor relationship.

## KPIs to establish baseline for

Before deployment, capture baseline numbers:

- Monthly inbound call volume.
- Miss rate (percentage of calls not helped live).
- After-hours capture rate.
- Booking rate (bookings per inbound qualified call).
- No-show rate (if applicable).
- Front-desk hours spent on phone.
- Average response time on new-lead calls.

Post-deployment, compare against these baselines monthly.

## Common issues in the first 90 days

**Weeks 1 to 2:** callers uncertain about the AI, some hangups when they realize. Fades as tone gets tuned.

**Weeks 3 to 4:** unusual caller patterns not covered in initial script. Standard tuning territory.

**Weeks 5 to 8:** integration edge cases surface. Rare CRM patterns need mapping adjustment.

**Weeks 9 to 12:** scope creep temptation. Resist adding features until steady-state is solid.

## What determines a successful 90-day arc

- Aggressive monitoring in weeks 1 to 4.
- Fast iteration on identified issues.
- Clear ownership on your side.
- Willingness to invest in ongoing tuning.
- Honest KPI tracking against baseline.

Deployments with all five characteristics reliably produce transformational results by day 90. Deployments missing any of the five tend to underperform.

## What to do at day 90

If the deployment is meeting or exceeding your ROI targets: renew, potentially expand scope.

If the deployment is underperforming: honest conversation with your vendor about why. Fixable issues get fixed. Fundamentally wrong-tier deployments get restructured.

If the deployment is a complete miss: leave without penalty (any vendor requiring long-term lock-in without an exit is a bad vendor). Try a different vendor at the right tier next time.

## Where to go from here

If you are still evaluating: read the buyer's guide at /resources/guides.

If you have signed and are in deployment: this playbook lives on the wall of your ops team. Reference weekly through the first 90 days.

If you are past day 90 and successful: consider scope expansion. Outbound sequences, additional integrations, additional locations, or the full follow-up product typically deliver additional 20 to 40 percent gains on top of the initial deployment.
