Real AI receptionist ROI in production ranges from 8-day payback (HVAC after-hours emergency capture) to 90-day payback (medspa retention plus new-client booking). Typical mid-market service business: 2-4 weeks to break even, then 3-6x monthly agent cost in recovered revenue by month three. The AI receptionist category is drowning in ROI claims that do
not survive contact with a spreadsheet. "10x ROI." "Pays for
itself in 24 hours." "Triple your bookings." I have built the
model behind every deployment I ship and I run the actual numbers
on the year-one report card. Here is what real ROI looks like
across the portfolio, and the framework you can use to model your
own before you sign anything.

## The ROI formula I use before every deployment

Four inputs. Nothing exotic. If you can answer these four
questions for your business, you can model ROI to within 15
percent.

<Pull>
Missed calls per month × your close rate on answered calls ×
average booked-job value × 0.7 (realistic capture) = recoverable
monthly revenue.
</Pull>

Subtract monthly agent cost to get net gain. Divide monthly agent
cost by daily net gain to get payback in days.

The 0.7 multiplier is the honest one. An AI receptionist does
not capture every previously-missed call, some are wrong
numbers, some are spam, some are callers who hung up before
saying what they wanted. The 70 percent recovery rate is what I
see consistently across verticals when the deployment is scoped
correctly. Vendors quoting 90+ percent are cherry-picking their
best week.

## Payback windows by vertical, from actual deployments

Not marketing claims. These are numbers pulled from year-one
reports on real customers.

### HVAC: 8 to 14 days

Highest ROI vertical, driven by after-hours emergency capture. A
shop doing 800 inbound calls per month at 30 percent missed
recovers 168 calls per month post-deployment. Average booked-job
value $340, 55 percent close rate → $31,416 per month recovered.
Agent cost $1,400 per month → payback in ~1.3 days of that
month's cash flow. Not "cash flow break even", payback of the
whole month's bill in a day and a half.

### Dental: 3 to 6 weeks

Booking-density vertical. Recovery is on rebookings, new-patient
intake, and no-show reduction rather than after-hours emergency.
Solid math but slower payback because dental is not emergency-weighted.

### Real estate teams (5+ agents): 4 to 8 weeks

Value per recovered lead is high, but volume is lower. One
recovered listing typically covers the agent for the year.
Payback depends on when that recovered listing lands in the
window.

### Personal injury law firms: 2 to 6 weeks

Sensitive to after-hours capture. One signed matter typically
covers agent cost for many months. Payback comes down to when
the first case signs.

### Med spas: 4 to 10 weeks

Booking capture is fast. The retention layer (package renewals)
takes longer to compound. Full ROI shows in month 3 to 6.

### Solo operators (all verticals): 2 to 4 months

Lower call volume means lower absolute recovery. Still positive
ROI but longer payback.

### Home services (plumbing, electrical, roofing): 2 to 4 weeks

Similar dynamics to HVAC but somewhat less emergency-weighted.
Still fast.

## The variable that swings the math most

It is not the agent's per-minute cost. That has dropped by more
than half in the last 24 months and will keep dropping.

The variable that swings ROI is your current missed-call rate.
That is the numerator in the whole equation.

- Business missing 15 percent of calls: modest ROI, might not
  justify tier 2 spend.
- Business missing 30 percent of calls: strong ROI, payback in
  weeks.
- Business missing 50+ percent of calls (common in service
  operations without dedicated front desk): transformational ROI,
  payback in days.

Before evaluating any vendor, measure your real miss rate. Pull
your carrier's call detail records for the last 30 days and count
the unanswered ones. Then listen to 30 answered calls and score
how many actually got helped versus put on hold and dropped, sent
to voicemail from the queue, or ended without a next step. That
is your true miss rate. Most operators are shocked by it.

## What "capture rate" actually means

Vendors love to quote capture rates. Here is what those numbers
actually contain.

- **Total inbound answered by the agent:** 100 percent, if
  configured correctly. Every call gets answered. That is the
  easy number.
- **Calls where a real conversation happened:** Typically 85 to
  92 percent. Some callers hang up when they hear an AI. Some
  numbers are non-conversational (wrong numbers, spam).
- **Calls where a defined business outcome was captured** (lead
  intake completed, appointment booked, transfer executed): 65
  to 80 percent of previously-missed calls. This is the number
  that matters for ROI.
- **Calls where the outcome converts to booked revenue:** Depends
  on your close rate. 40 to 70 percent of captured outcomes
  become booked jobs.

When I write ROI models, I use the third number (65 to 80
percent) as the base rate. When vendors show you 95+ percent
capture rates, they are usually quoting the first number.

## The savings side of the ROI

Recovered revenue is one half. The other half is what you stop
paying for.

### Answering service replacement

If you currently pay $600 to $1,400 per month for a human
answering service that handles after-hours coverage, an AI
receptionist typically replaces it. Net savings around $500 to
$1,000 per month.

### Front-desk hours reduced

For businesses with a front-desk person whose primary role is
phone coverage, AI receptionist can reduce hours needed. Not
eliminate, someone still needs to handle walk-ins, complex
scenarios, and the physical office, but the phone-only
coverage overhead drops. Typical savings: 8 to 15 hours per week
of front-desk time redeployed.

### Missed-lead follow-up staffing

Some operations have a dedicated staffer doing missed-call
follow-up during the day. If the AI captures those calls in
real time, that role becomes unnecessary.

### Call center seat count

For businesses running actual call centers, per-seat costs of
$28 to $42 per hour compare against $0.10 to $0.19 per minute
for AI. The economics favor automation for high-volume routine
work.

## The ROI patterns that break down

Not every deployment produces the ROI the model projects. Here
are the patterns I have seen where the math falls short.

- **Scope creep.** Operator wanted the agent to also handle
  complex intake that should have been human. Fixes to scope
  after go-live cost time and disrupt the tuning cycle.
- **Skipped discovery week.** The agent does not speak your
  callers' vocabulary and callers hang up more than expected.
- **Wrong tier.** Tier 1 templated bot on a business that needed
  tier 2 depth. Capture rate stays below 50 percent and ROI does
  not clear the threshold.
- **No monitoring in month one.** Nobody listens to flagged
  calls, patterns of failure go unfixed, retention on the
  deployment falls off.

The ROI model assumes the deployment is done right. When the
deployment is done wrong, the model does not apply.

## The framework for modeling your own ROI

1. **Pull 30 days of call records.** Actual carrier data, not
   estimates.
2. **Score your real miss rate.** Answered live and helped
   versus everything else.
3. **Look up your average booked-job value from your last 12
   months of AR.**
4. **Estimate your close rate on answered qualified calls.** If
   you do not track this, default to 40 percent for services and
   30 percent for retail.
5. **Multiply calls × miss rate × close rate × job value ×
   0.7.**
6. **Subtract agent cost.**
7. **If the result is positive by 2x or more, the deployment
   works economically.**

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the ROI faster in some months than others?**
Yes. Seasonal businesses see stronger ROI in peak season. HVAC
sees its biggest recovery during heat waves and cold snaps.
Landscaping sees it in spring. Roofing after storms. Model
average annualized ROI, not peak-week ROI.

**What if I already have a great answering service?**
Then your ROI math includes the savings from replacing it plus
the incremental capture rate the AI improves over the human
service. In most cases the AI recovers more calls faster.

**Does the ROI hold when voice AI prices drop further?**
Yes. Per-minute cost is a fraction of monthly agent bills.
Further drops improve ROI marginally but do not change the
fundamental math.

**What about qualitative benefits?**
Real but hard to model. Owner peace of mind (phone always
answered), reduced staff burnout on phone coverage, professional
first impression on new-customer inbound. I do not include these
in ROI models but operators consistently cite them as retention
factors.

**When should I not expect strong ROI?**
Businesses under 150 calls per month, businesses where the
owner personally answers every call, businesses with fundamental
operational issues that phone automation will not solve.
