<Answer q="What do AI agents do for lawyers?">
An AI agent for lawyers handles first-touch intake on new-matter phone calls, capturing name, matter type, jurisdiction, incident date, opposing party, and urgency, while flagging potential conflicts against the firm's client database. It books qualified prospects onto the attorney's consultation calendar and declines or refers cases outside the firm's practice areas. It never offers legal advice, never quotes fees on retainer, and never discusses the merits of a matter.
</Answer>

The firms I have shipped voice agents into range from four-partner
personal injury shops to solo immigration practitioners. The
common problem is the same. Attorneys who close new clients on
consultation calls cannot afford to be the ones answering the
initial intake line, and yet the initial call is where the
qualified matter is either won or lost. Voice AI has settled into
this gap cleanly over the last two years. It has also created a
new set of ethics considerations that I will not skip over. If
you are a managing attorney evaluating this, read the ethics
section before anything else.

## The intake problem most firms describe

When I run a discovery call with a firm, I ask for two things: the
last thirty days of inbound call logs, and a list of the practice
areas the firm actually wants new matters in. The gap between
those two lists is where the leakage lives.

- Answer rate on the general intake line during business hours:
  55 to 68 percent for small firms, higher for firms with
  dedicated intake staff.
- After-hours answer rate: near zero unless the firm pays for a
  legal-specific answering service, which typically runs $600 to
  $1,400 per month and still involves a callback delay.
- Median time-to-first-attorney-touch on qualified matters: 4 to
  12 hours.
- Estimated share of viable matters lost to competing firms in
  that window: personal injury and family law report 25 to 40
  percent. Immigration and small business are lower.

For personal injury specifically, that first-touch window is
brutal. PI callers dial three or four firms and hire whoever calls
back first with a real person on the phone. Every minute past the
first touch is loss.

## What an AI agent actually does for a law firm

I want to describe this concretely because "AI agent" gets used
loosely. Here is what mine do in production.

- **Answer every inbound within one ring, 24/7.** No IVR tree, no
  practice-area menu. The agent identifies the firm and asks what
  brought the caller in.
- **Classify the matter type.** Personal injury, family, criminal
  defense, estate planning, immigration, real estate, business
  formation, employment. The classification determines the intake
  branch that runs next.
- **Run a jurisdiction check.** State, sometimes county. Firms
  licensed in specific jurisdictions cannot open matters outside
  them. The agent gates on this before proceeding.
- **Capture the intake data.** For PI: incident date, injury type,
  medical treatment status, insurance carrier if known, opposing
  party if known. For family: filing status, opposing party,
  children involved yes-or-no, urgency. Each practice area has a
  tuned intake form.
- **Flag potential conflicts.** The agent queries the firm's case
  management system (Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther, Filevine) for
  opposing-party name matches. A soft conflict flag routes the
  call to a paralegal or intake specialist for verification
  rather than booking.
- **Book the consultation.** Qualified, non-conflicted callers get
  a consultation slot on the appropriate attorney's calendar. The
  agent sends a confirmation SMS with the meeting link or address.
- **Follow up on no-shows and pending consultations.** Two-touch
  sequence on no-show consults, and a single-touch reminder 24
  hours before scheduled consults.

## The ethics line I never let it cross

This section is the one I insist every managing attorney read
before signing on. There is no reasonable version of this scope
that includes any of the following.

- **No legal advice.** Ever. Not "you probably have a case," not
  "the statute of limitations is X in your state," not "you should
  file within Y days." The agent explicitly declines and offers a
  consultation.
- **No fee quotes on retainer.** Fee arrangements are attorney
  conversations. The agent may reference publicly-published
  consultation fees ("the initial consultation is $250, applied
  to your matter if you retain") but not retainer amounts.
- **No case-strength opinions.** "That sounds like a strong case"
  is a compliance violation. The agent stays neutral on facts.
- **No prediction of outcomes.** "You should win" or "you should
  settle for X" never leave the agent's mouth.
- **Prompt disclosure that it is an AI.** The agent identifies as
  a virtual intake assistant on the greeting, not later. In some
  state bars, this disclosure is either explicit or trending
  toward explicit, I default to on regardless.

Every firm I deploy for gets a "hard stop" list that includes any
practice-area-specific things the local bar has warned about. That
list is the difference between an intake tool and a bar complaint.

Clio is the most common request and it is the fastest to
integrate. Its API is well-documented and its webhook behavior is
predictable. MyCase and PracticePanther both work but the
webhook layer is quirkier. Filevine is heavier to integrate but
worth it for personal injury firms, its intake field structure
matches how PI matters actually flow.

For calendar, most attorneys live in Google Calendar or Outlook.
For SMS, Twilio with a firm-owned number so consultation
confirmations come from a recognizable local area code. For call
recording storage, I default to a 30-day retention policy in a
signed S3 bucket with access limited to the intake team and the
managing attorney.

## When the conflict flag catches something

The conflict-check layer is worth explaining because it is the
feature that saves firms from real trouble.

The agent takes the opposing party name during intake and runs
a fuzzy match against the case management system. If a match
returns, the agent does not surface it to the caller, that would
be a confidentiality violation. It simply routes the call to the
intake supervisor or paralegal with a flag: "possible conflict,
opposing party X matches prior client Y, please verify before
consultation booking." A human handles the disposition from
there. This layer typically catches 2 to 5 potential conflicts
per hundred intakes, and any single one avoided pays for the
year of the agent.

## Compliance considerations I would not skip

1. **State bar disclosures.** Some state bars have issued opinions
   on AI-assisted client intake. Read your jurisdiction's opinion
   before deploying and mirror its language in the greeting.
2. **Recording consent.** Audible notification in the opening
   greeting. Two-party consent states require it; I default to on
   everywhere else.
3. **Confidentiality.** Call recordings and transcripts must be
   treated as prospective-client confidential from the moment of
   the call. Access-controlled storage is not optional.
4. **Conflict of interest.** The conflict-check layer is not
   optional. Deploying without it in a firm with any real client
   base is a preventable ethics risk.
5. **Trust account discussion.** The agent may not discuss
   retainer amounts, trust account requirements, or fee agreement
   terms. Those are attorney conversations.

## What the deployment looks like

Legal deployments go live in 72 hours from kickoff, the same as
HVAC or real estate. The difference is that your own ethics and
compliance sign-off is scheduled before kickoff, so practice-area
branches and state bar requirements are settled before the build
starts.

- **Hours 0-24:** Discovery and script draft. Review call logs,
  catalog practice areas, capture the "hard stop" list, review
  any state bar opinions, then draft per-practice-area intake
  branches, jurisdiction gates, conflict-check logic, and
  transfer conditions.
- **Hours 24-48:** CRM integration, calendar wiring, conflict-check
  webhook.
- **Hours 48-72:** Test calls. Full mock-caller battery, I run 40
  to 60 calls including hostile callers, callers seeking advice,
  callers with obvious conflicts. Your sign-off, then go-live.
- **After go-live:** Silent monitor for 72 hours minimum.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a violation of the unauthorized practice of law?**
Not when scoped correctly. The agent takes intake data and books
consultations. It does not advise, does not opine on merits, and
does not quote retainer amounts. Every state bar that has weighed
in has accepted this scope with proper disclosure.

**What about attorney-client privilege on the call recording?**
The recording is treated as prospective-client confidential from
the first word. It is not privileged in the technical sense until
representation is established, but I treat it with the same
handling protocols regardless. Access is limited to intake staff
and the managing attorney.

**Can the agent take payment for the consultation?**
Yes, if the firm charges for consultations. It can trigger a Stripe
or LawPay link over SMS. It does not take card numbers over voice.

**What happens if the caller becomes hostile or emotional?**
The agent has an escalation branch. If the caller starts crying,
becomes hostile, or asks to speak to a human, the agent bridges
to your intake supervisor or leaves a priority callback flag
depending on time of day.

**What if the caller wants to speak Spanish?**
Voice models handle Spanish-English switching cleanly. If your
firm serves a heavily Spanish-speaking population, the agent
detects language on the greeting and continues in the caller's
preferred language.

## When an AI agent is the wrong fit for a firm

- Solo practices doing fewer than 30 intakes per month. Handle
  those manually.
- Firms where every intake is inherently unique (complex tax
  litigation, high-stakes patent) and there is no repeatable
  intake pattern to script.
- Firms where the managing attorney is philosophically opposed to
  AI on intake. This is not a fit problem I try to solve.
