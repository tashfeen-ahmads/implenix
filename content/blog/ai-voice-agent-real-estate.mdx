<Answer q="What does an AI voice agent do for a real estate team?">
An AI voice agent for real estate answers inbound lead calls 24/7, qualifies buyer or seller intent (price range, timeline, financing status, motivation), books tours or listing consultations on the licensed agent's calendar, and runs multi-touch follow-up on cold leads. It does not negotiate offers, give pricing opinions on specific homes, or handle anything that requires a real-estate license. That line matters, I will not build past it.
</Answer>

I get asked "will AI replace real estate agents?" on every
discovery call. The short answer is no. The longer answer is that
AI voice agents already handle the parts of the job most licensed
agents actively dislike, cold-lead follow-up, intake on new
Zillow inquiries, showing coordination, missed-call recovery, and
that work being handled reliably is what a solo agent or a small
team actually needs. This piece is written for the team lead
evaluating whether a voice AI belongs on their inbound line, and
it is written from the deployments I have actually shipped, not
from vendor pitch decks.

## The real estate call log I usually see

When I run a discovery call with a team lead or a solo producer,
the first thing I ask for is the call log from their last thirty
days. Here is what I typically find, across teams of all sizes.

- Inbound answer rate on IDX/Zillow lead calls: 35 to 48 percent.
  Most inbound rings while the agent is at a showing, in a listing
  presentation, or driving.
- Time-to-first-response on Zillow leads that go to voicemail:
  median 71 minutes. Zillow's own data shows conversion drops
  sharply past five minutes.
- Missed calls per week that never got a callback attempted: 14
  to 22 percent.
- Cold leads (three-plus months old) actively being worked: near
  zero. Almost nobody re-touches those.

Real estate is not a phone-volume industry the way HVAC is. It is
a phone-latency industry. The lead does not need to be answered
five hundred times, it needs to be answered within five minutes
the first time. Missing that window is what costs deals.

## What an AI voice agent handles for a real estate team

I will list the actual production use cases, in order of the ROI
I have measured.

- **Inbound lead intake.** Agent answers within one ring, greets
  by team name, asks buy-or-sell, captures price range, timeline,
  ZIP or neighborhood, and financing status if buyer. Books a
  15-minute follow-up call on the licensed agent's calendar.
- **Showing coordination.** For teams that run a lot of showings,
  the agent handles inbound "can I see the house on Elm Street
  Saturday" requests, checks calendar and MLS status, and books.
- **Missed-call recovery.** When a call goes unanswered, the agent
  auto-calls the number back within 30 seconds with "hey, we saw
  your call, how can I help." Recovers about 60 to 70 percent of
  callers who otherwise would have moved on.
- **Cold-lead reactivation.** Sequence of two voice touches and
  three SMS touches spread over 14 days on aged leads. Recovers
  roughly 4 to 7 percent as active conversations.
- **Zillow / IDX form follow-up.** New form submission triggers a
  voice call within 90 seconds, followed by SMS if unanswered.
  This is the highest-ROI single behavior for buyer teams.

## Where a licensed agent still wins

I want to be blunt here because I have watched vendors overpromise
in real estate and it damages the trust of the whole category.

- **Offer negotiations.** Not the AI's job. Not now, not soon.
- **Pricing conversations on specific homes.** The agent can share
  the list price. Anything beyond that, comps, condition, seller
  motivation, negotiation strategy, goes to the licensed agent.
- **Contract discussions.** Any conversation about terms belongs
  with a licensed human. The agent explicitly declines and books a
  callback.
- **Anything that could constitute practicing real estate without
  a license.** I keep the agent tightly scoped away from "should I
  offer" and "is this a good deal" questions.

Every deployment I ship gets a "hard stop" list, questions the
agent will not answer, no matter how the caller phrases them.
That list is what keeps you and your brokerage out of trouble.

The CRM is where the real estate deployment succeeds or fails.

I have shipped voice agents wired to Follow Up Boss, kvCORE,
BoomTown, Sierra Interactive, and generic HubSpot. Follow Up Boss
is the fastest integration path in real estate today, and I say
that regardless of vendor preference. kvCORE has richer IDX data
but its webhook behavior is inconsistent under load, I have to
add retry logic around it.

For calendar, most teams live in Google Calendar. Some brokerages
use Outlook 365 via Microsoft Graph. Both are stable. Calendly and
Acuity are the most common third-party calendar layers and both
work fine.

## Cold-lead reactivation is the sleeper use case

The reactivation sequence is the thing that surprises real estate
operators most. Here is how it runs.

- **Day 0:** Voice call. If unanswered, agent leaves a 20-second
  voicemail (compliant, identifies itself as a virtual assistant
  from the team) and follows with an opt-in-compliant SMS.
- **Day 3:** SMS with a low-friction question, "Are you still
  looking in the [neighborhood] area, or did your search shift?"
- **Day 7:** Voice call. If answered, qualifies fresh intent and
  books a callback with the licensed agent.
- **Day 14:** Final SMS with an opt-out.

On lead lists over six months old with no touches in that window,
this sequence recovers 4 to 7 percent as active conversations, which for a team sitting on 800 dead leads is 32 to 56 revived
prospects at zero incremental cost. That is more listing pipeline
than most teams generate from a month of Zillow.

## The compliance layer I insist on

Real estate has three regulatory considerations that shape the
deployment.

1. **TCPA.** Any AI-initiated call to a mobile phone requires prior
   express consent. Inbound calls the caller placed are fine. Cold
   outbound from a purchased list is not. Reactivation sequences
   on prior clients or opt-in leads are the safe zone.
2. **State recording consent.** Two-party consent states require
   an audible recording notification. I include it in the greeting.
3. **Fair Housing.** The agent's script must avoid any language
   that could be construed as steering. The script gets a Fair
   Housing review before go-live, this is standard, and it is
   fast, but it is not skippable.
4. **License disclosure.** The agent identifies itself as a virtual
   assistant from the team, not as a licensed agent. Never claim
   what it is not.

## What the deployment looks like

Real estate deployments go live in 72 hours from kickoff, like
every other vertical we ship. Intake patterns in real estate are
highly repeatable, which makes it one of the cleanest builds.

- **Hours 0-24:** Discovery and script draft. Listen to recent
  inbound calls, read the team's current lead-intake script,
  capture the "hard stop" list, then draft the buyer branch,
  seller branch, showing branch, reactivation branch, transfer
  rules.
- **Hours 24-48:** CRM + calendar wiring. Lead scoring, tag
  assignment, agent-of-record routing.
- **Hours 48-72:** Test calls, your sign-off, and go-live.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will AI replace real estate agents?**
No. The parts of the job that require a license, judgment about
markets, negotiation strategy, and long-term client relationships
are not going anywhere. What the AI does is take the intake and
qualification layer off your desk so you can focus on the deals
that are actually moving.

**What happens if a caller asks a question the agent should not
answer?**
The agent declines cleanly and offers a callback with the licensed
agent. My script for this is: "That is a great question for
[agent name], she can walk you through the specifics. Want me to
have her call you in the next hour?" Callers accept this without
friction.

**Can the agent handle Spanish-speaking callers?**
Yes. Voice models handle Spanish-English switching in most markets
cleanly. If your team serves a market with heavy multilingual
volume, the agent gets a language-detection greeting and continues
in the caller's language.

**What if a lead is already in the CRM?**
The agent checks the CRM on inbound and greets by name if there is
a match, and pulls the existing agent-of-record so the callback
gets scheduled with the right person.

**Does it work with dialer platforms like Mojo or PhoneBurner?**
Those are outbound tools. For outbound reactivation the AI voice
agent replaces them entirely. For live prospecting where you want
to be the one talking, it does not.

## Where an AI voice agent is the wrong fit

- Solo agent doing under 10 inbound leads per week.
- Team where the owner is philosophically opposed to any
  automation on the phone. This is not a technical problem, it is
  a fit problem, and I respect it.
- Brokerages that require every inbound touch to be logged in a
  proprietary phone system with no API surface. The wiring
  becomes brittle and I would rather decline than ship
  something fragile.
