<Answer q="How do I calculate the cost of missed calls?">
The formula is four inputs multiplied together: monthly inbound call volume × miss rate × close rate on answered calls × average booked-job value = current monthly loss from missed inbound. To convert that ceiling into a realistic ROI estimate for an AI receptionist or added answering capacity, multiply by 0.7 (the realistic capture rate on previously-missed calls). Most local-service businesses discover their missed-call cost is 5-20x what they had guessed.
</Answer>

Almost every operator I run a discovery call with thinks they
know what their missed calls cost. Almost every one of them is
wrong by a factor of 5 to 20. The number they had in their head
was a gut estimate; the number from the actual formula is much
larger and much more actionable. This is the formula I run
before every deployment, with honest guidance on how to
estimate each input from a business that has never measured it
before.

## Who this formula is for

Owner-operators and ops leads at any business where the phone
is the primary inbound channel. The math runs cleanest for
local services (HVAC, plumbing, dental, roofing, veterinary,
med spa), and it works for legal, mortgage, real estate, home
services, and any operation where a booked call turns into
recurring revenue.

If your phone rings fewer than 30 times a month, this is not
your bottleneck. Skip this piece.

## The four variables

<Pull>
Calls per month × miss rate × close rate on answered × average
client value = current monthly missed-call cost.
</Pull>

None of these variables need to be measured perfectly. They
need to be measured within about 20% for the framework to be
useful. Better to have a rough working number today than a
perfect number in six weeks.

### Variable 1: monthly inbound call volume

The count of inbound calls your business receives per month.

**How to get it right:** pull the call detail records from your
carrier. Every VoIP provider (RingCentral, Nextiva, 8x8) and
traditional carrier surfaces this in their admin dashboard. It
is the actual number of times your phone rang.

**Common estimation errors:**
- Confusing "calls handled by staff" with "total inbound." The
  difference is usually 30-50%.
- Ignoring calls that went straight to voicemail without a ring.
- Missing after-hours volume because your dashboard defaults to
  business hours only.

**Shortcut when you can't get carrier data:** count inbound
for one full week (Monday through Sunday) and multiply by 4.3.
Not perfect, but usable.

### Variable 2: miss rate

The percentage of inbound calls where the caller did NOT reach
a live human who helped them accomplish what they called about.

This is the variable operators get most wrong. They report
"we answer most of our calls." What they mean is "we answer
most of the calls we hear ring."

**How to measure it honestly:** pull one week of call
recordings. Randomly sample 30 calls. Score each one:

- **"Real answer" (1 point):** live human picked up within
  three rings, held a real conversation, and either resolved
  the call or transferred cleanly.
- **"Miss" (0 points):** anything else. Voicemail with or
  without a message left. Hold music followed by abandonment.
  Answered but transferred and dropped. Answered but caller
  gave up during the intake.

Total misses / 30 = your real miss rate.

**What real numbers look like:**
- Well-staffed dental practice: 25-40% miss rate.
- Solo HVAC operator running from a truck: 55-75%.
- Multi-line real estate team: 40-60% on new IDX leads.
- Small law firm without dedicated intake: 45-65%.

Most operators are shocked. If you have never measured this,
prepare to be shocked.

### Variable 3: close rate on answered qualified calls

The percentage of qualified inbound calls (people who actually
want your service, not spam or wrong numbers) that convert to
booked jobs, signed matters, or paid engagements.

**Where to get this:** last month's booked-jobs count divided
by last month's inbound qualified conversation count.

**If you do not measure it, defaults by industry:**
- HVAC / plumbing / electrical: 45-65%
- Dental new-patient: 35-55%
- Law firm PI intake: 12-20% (sign rate)
- Real estate buyer leads: 8-15%
- Med spa booking: 55-75%
- General retail service: 25-40%

Use the lower end of the range if your operation is chaotic or
your close-follow-up is weak. Use the higher end if you have
tight processes.

### Variable 4: average client value

The revenue from a booked job or signed customer. Use lifetime
value where recurring revenue matters; use single-job average
where it does not.

**How to get it:** pull last 12 months of AR, divide by number
of booked jobs. That is your average.

**Adjustments to consider:**
- **Repeat customers:** most service businesses have 30-50% of
  revenue from repeat customers. If you have that, use lifetime
  value, not per-job.
- **Referrals from each customer:** a new customer who refers
  two more is worth much more than the first job's revenue.
- **Membership or contract customers:** annualize the contract
  value.

**Simple heuristic:** if your average job is $340 and your
average customer stays 3 years with 4 jobs per year, your real
average client value is closer to $4,000 than $340. Use the
larger number.

## Worked example: an HVAC shop

Real numbers from an actual discovery call, lightly redacted.

- Monthly inbound: 780 calls (from carrier records).
- Miss rate: 32% (from a random sample of 40 recent calls).
- Close rate on answered qualified calls: 55%.
- Average booked-job value: $340 (single job, no LTV adjustment
  because most were emergency dispatches).

**Missed-call cost math:**
- 780 × 0.32 = 250 missed calls per month.
- 250 × 0.55 = 137 lost booked jobs per month.
- 137 × $340 = **$46,580 per month in unbooked pipeline**.

Owner's guess before running the math: "maybe five thousand a
month, tops."

Reality: ten times the guess.

## Worked example: a personal injury law firm

- Monthly inbound: 180 new-matter intake calls.
- Miss rate: 41% (after-hours capture near zero, business-hour
  miss rate 25%).
- Sign rate on qualified intake conversations: 15%.
- Average signed case value: $9,800.

**Missed-matter cost math:**
- 180 × 0.41 = 74 missed intakes per month.
- 74 × 0.15 = 11 lost signed cases per month.
- 11 × $9,800 = **$107,800 per month in lost case value**.

At those numbers, an AI intake agent that captures half of
previously-missed intakes returns $53,900/month, many times its
monthly cost. Payback
in first week of operation.

## The realistic recovery multiplier

The formula above gives you the ceiling, every missed call
turned into booked revenue. That is not what actually happens
even with a great deployment.

**Realistic recovery on previously-missed calls: 65-80%.** The
rest are wrong numbers, spam, callers who hung up before
saying what they wanted, callers who abandoned when they heard
AI.

For working ROI numbers, multiply the ceiling by 0.7. That
gives you a defensible number to plan around.

For the HVAC example: $46,580 × 0.7 = **$32,606/month in
recoverable revenue**, a small fraction of which goes to the
agent.

## What the number does NOT include

The formula captures direct revenue from booked jobs. It does
not include:

- **Reputation damage** from unreturned voicemails (real but
  hard to quantify).
- **Staff morale** costs from the front desk knowing they are
  missing calls.
- **Owner peace of mind** value from knowing the phone is
  covered.
- **Referral revenue** from customers who would have referred
  after a good first experience.
- **After-hours emergency capture** in verticals where
  emergency response value exceeds normal booked-job value.

Every one of these adds to the real number. The formula is a
conservative floor, not a ceiling.

## The estimation shortcuts operators can use today

If you cannot pull carrier data or listen to recordings, three
quick shortcuts get you close:

1. **Ask your front desk what percentage of calls they think
   they miss.** Take their answer and add 30 percentage points.
   That is closer to reality.
2. **Count how many voicemails you actually returned yesterday.**
   Divide by how many voicemails were left. Most operators
   discover they return under 60%.
3. **Look at your Google Business Profile call reports.** They
   surface unanswered call counts if you have call tracking
   enabled.

None of these are as accurate as the formula, but they get you
oriented in minutes.

## When the missed-call cost math does NOT justify a deployment

Not every business has ROI positive on AI voice. The math
does not work if:
- Under 100 inbound calls per month (fixed monthly cost does
  not amortize).
- Miss rate under 15% (low leak to capture).
- Average job value under $50 with no LTV.
- Business is one-person and the owner already answers every
  call personally.

If your math produces a monthly missed-call cost under $2,500,
handle the calls manually and revisit when volume grows.

## Frequently asked questions

**What if my inbound is mostly repeat customers?**
Use lifetime value, not per-job. Missed calls from repeat
customers cost you the same recurring revenue as if a new
customer had been lost.

**What if I don't know my close rate?**
Use the industry default range (see above). Round conservatively.

**Should I include after-hours calls in the volume?**
Absolutely. After-hours is where the largest capture
opportunity typically lives.

**Does the formula work for outbound sales?**
Not directly. This is inbound math. Outbound sales cost
analysis uses different frameworks.

**How often should I re-run the formula?**
Once at deployment scoping, once at 90-day post-deployment
review, then annually. Business changes, formula inputs change.
