<Answer q="How much does an AI voice agent cost?">
An AI voice agent costs between $50 per month for a DIY templated bot and $2,500+ per month for a fully-custom deployment. The three practical tiers are: DIY/templated ($50 to $200/mo, no setup), platform + agency ($500 to $1,500/mo plus $2,000 to $8,000 setup), and fully custom ($1,000 to $2,500/mo plus $10,000 to $50,000 setup). Anyone quoting an "unlimited" bundle below $400/mo is selling a template, real deployments have a floor around $700 to $900 all-in.
</Answer>

The question I get on every discovery call is some version of
"what should I expect to pay?" I have priced hundreds of these
now, in every combination of scale and complexity. This is what
the real cost model looks like when you take the vendor marketing
copy off.

## The three cost layers in any voice AI deployment

Every deployment, at every tier, has three cost layers. Understand
these three and you can read any vendor pricing page correctly.

### 1. Build fee (one-time)

The upfront work to configure, script, integrate, and test the
agent for your specific operation. Ranges from zero (templated
tier 1) to $50,000+ (enterprise custom).

### 2. Monthly platform + telephony

The recurring cost to keep the agent live. Includes voice model
inference, speech-to-text, text-to-speech, telephony carrier
fees, orchestration platform, and hosting. Usually billed as a
platform fee plus per-minute charges.

### 3. Ongoing tuning and optimization

Someone reviews flagged calls, updates scripts, adjusts routing.
Often optional at tier 1, standard at tier 2, always included at
tier 3. Ranges from $0 to $2,000+ per month.

Anyone showing you a single "monthly price" number without
breaking out these three layers is hiding something. Ask for the
breakdown.

## Tier 1: DIY templated ($50 to $200 per month)

**Build fee:** $0 to $500 (usually included in signup).
**Monthly:** $50 to $200.
**Tuning:** DIY, unpaid.

**What you actually get:** A self-serve voice bot with a
templated script layer. You configure your business hours, paste
in your greeting, connect Google Calendar, and go live. Voice
quality is usually one generation behind the top platforms.
Integrations are limited to a short list of common ones. Script
variations are shallow.

**Where this makes sense:** Solo operators, single-location
shops, under 200 calls per month, straightforward booking-only
workflow, no compliance sensitivity.

**Where this fails:** Multi-intent inbound (booking + support +
inquiry), complex integrations, industry compliance requirements,
anything above 500 calls per month.

## Tier 2: Platform + agency ($500 to $1,500 per month)

**Build fee:** $2,000 to $8,000.
**Monthly:** $500 to $1,500 (platform + telephony + minutes).
**Tuning:** $400 to $900 per month optional.

**What you actually get:** A voice agent built on a professional
platform (Retell, Vapi, Bland, or similar) with an agency doing
the discovery, scripting, integration, ongoing QA, and script
optimization. This is where the middle market of the industry
sits and where most real deployments live.

**Where this makes sense:** Multi-location small businesses,
service operators doing 200 to 1,000 calls per month, any shop
with meaningful integrations (ServiceTitan, Clio, Boulevard),
regulated industries needing scoped disclosures.

**Where this fails:** Very high call volume (over 3,000 minutes
per month becomes expensive), enterprise integrations requiring
custom SDKs, unique compliance layers.

## Tier 3: Fully custom ($1,000 to $2,500+ per month)

**Build fee:** $10,000 to $50,000+.
**Monthly:** $1,000 to $2,500+ (platform + telephony at volume
rates).
**Tuning:** $800 to $2,000 per month, always included.

**What you actually get:** A fully custom voice agent built for
your specific compliance, integration, and voice-brand
requirements. Ownership of the deployment IP. Deep integration
with proprietary systems. Custom voice cloning if desired. Fully
staffed QA and optimization.

**Where this makes sense:** Multi-location chains (10+
locations), enterprise SMBs, regulated verticals with specific
compliance layers (HIPAA-scoped medspa, credit unions,
enterprise legal).

**Where this fails:** Small operators who buy up the tier
"because it sounds professional." The overhead is not worth it
below meaningful scale.

## The per-minute reality no one discusses

At all three tiers, your true unit cost is per-minute. Here is
what actually goes into that number.

Real all-in cost per minute in 2026 sits between $0.10 and $0.19
depending on model choice, telephony provider, and platform
markup. This has dropped significantly (about 60 percent) in the
last 24 months and will continue to drop.

For a business doing 2,000 inbound minutes per month, the raw
voice cost is $200 to $380. Everything above that in a monthly
bill covers platform, integrations, QA, and margin. That framing
tells you what your dollars are actually buying.

## The hidden costs I have seen bite operators

- **Overage on minutes bundles.** "Unlimited" always has a cap
  in the fine print, and the overage rate is usually punitive.
  Read the terms.
- **Integration change fees.** Some vendors charge per additional
  integration after the first. Ask upfront.
- **Custom voice / brand voice fees.** Cloning a specific voice
  runs $500 to $2,000 as a one-time fee at tier 2, sometimes
  monthly at tier 3.
- **Transcript storage and retrieval.** Some platforms charge for
  historical transcript access beyond 30 days. Ask.
- **After-hours support surcharges.** If your operation runs
  24/7 and needs vendor support at 3 AM, some contracts charge
  extra.
- **Cancellation fees or minimum contract periods.** Enterprise
  contracts frequently include these. Read before signing.

## What the payback math actually looks like

Cost matters only relative to what you get back. Here is the
framework I walk every operator through.

<Pull>
Current missed-call rate × monthly inbound call volume × average
booked-job value × close rate on answered calls = current monthly
loss.
</Pull>

For an HVAC shop doing 800 inbound calls at 30 percent missed,
average booked-job value $340, 55 percent close rate on answered:
current loss is $44,880 per month. A $1,400 per month tier 2
deployment that captures 70 percent of previously-missed calls
returns $31,416 per month. Payback in under two weeks.

For a solo dog groomer doing 80 calls at 20 percent missed,
average booking $65, 90 percent close rate: current loss is
$936 per month. A tier 1 solution at $89 per month capturing 60
percent of missed calls returns $562 per month. Payback in ten
days.

The math works at both ends. Where it does not work is when the
tier is mismatched to the operation.

## How to negotiate the deal correctly

- **Ask for the itemized quote.** Build fee, monthly platform,
  per-minute rate, integrations included, integrations extra, QA
  retainer. Anyone giving you a single number is hiding the
  breakdown.
- **Ask about term length and cancellation.** Month-to-month is
  ideal. Annual contracts with early-termination fees are common
  at tier 2 and above but often negotiable to quarterly.
- **Ask what happens on the day the agent fails.** Every
  deployment has a bad day at some point. What is the SLA? What
  is the fallback? Is there a human answering service backup?
- **Ask for two current-client references.** Speak to both. The
  reference-check call is the single highest-signal thing you can
  do before signing.

## Pricing red flags I would walk away from

- Unlimited minutes for under $300 per month.
- Setup fee under $500 for anything above tier 1.
- Multi-year contract required for a deployment where you are the
  test case in your industry.
- No specifics on integration approach for your specific software.
- Voice quality demo that sounds robotic and vendor's answer is
  "that is what your bot will sound like."
- No clear escalation-to-human path.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I really get a working voice agent for $50 per month?**
Yes, for a very narrow scope. A single-location solo business
doing simple booking-only calls can absolutely run on tier 1.
Beyond that scope, tier 1 breaks.

**Why is the setup fee so high at tier 2?**
Because the discovery week, script tuning, integration wiring,
and test-call battery are real work. That work is what
differentiates a real deployment from a templated bot with a
custom greeting. If a tier 2 vendor is quoting a setup fee under
$1,500, they are cutting corners on discovery or integration.

**How fast does pricing drop over time?**
Per-minute costs have dropped roughly 60 percent in the last
24 months. Expect another 40 to 50 percent drop over the next
24 months as voice models continue to compress. Platform and
build fees will not drop as fast.

**Should I buy annually to save on price?**
Only if you have done a 30-day pilot first. Do not commit to a
year of a vendor you have not run in production for at least a
month.

**What about "free tier" voice agents?**
Fine for tinkering. Not fine for anything you depend on for
revenue.

## Where to go from here

Get an itemized quote from two vendors at the tier that matches
your operation, do the payback math using your actual missed-call
volume, and pick the one whose reference calls actually sound
like your business. That is the whole selection process.

<Pull>
The right question is not "how cheap can I get this." The right
question is "at this price, does the ROI math work for my
operation?" If yes, buy. If not, do not.
</Pull>
