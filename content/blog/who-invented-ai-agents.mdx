<Answer q="Who invented AI agents?">
No single person invented AI agents. The idea grew over 70 years: Alan Turing and the 1956 Dartmouth workshop founded the field, SRI's Shakey robot (1966-1972) was the first machine to reason about its own actions, Wooldridge and Jennings defined the "intelligent agent" in 1995, Russell and Norvig built a textbook around it, and LLM-based agents arrived from 2022 onward.
</Answer>

The question "who invented AI agents" comes up in a lot of
introductory articles, and most answers are wrong or
oversimplified. The reality is that AI agents were not invented
by any one person or year, they are the product of seventy
years of accumulated research, with several pivotal moments
along the way. This is the honest short history, from someone
who ships modern AI agents for a living and reads the primary
research when the marketing gets loud.

## AI agent history at a glance

- **1950, Alan Turing.** Asked whether machines can think and proposed a test for it.
- **1956, McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, Shannon.** Dartmouth workshop founds AI as a field.
- **1966-1972, SRI (Shakey project).** First mobile robot to perceive, plan, and act on its own.
- **1973, Carl Hewitt.** Actor model: independent units that pass messages, a root of multi-agent systems.
- **1986, Rodney Brooks.** Subsumption architecture: behavior from layered sensing and acting.
- **1995, Wooldridge and Jennings.** Define an agent by autonomy, social ability, reactivity, pro-activeness.
- **1995, Russell and Norvig.** Textbook organizes all of AI around the intelligent agent.
- **2020, Lewis et al. (Facebook AI).** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
- **2022, Yao et al. (ReAct).** Language models that interleave reasoning and actions.
- **2023, AutoGPT, BabyAGI.** Viral open-source LLM agents pursuing multi-step goals.
- **2024-2025, Anthropic (MCP), Google (A2A).** Open standards for agent-to-tool and agent-to-agent connection.

## The foundational moment: Dartmouth, 1956

Six years earlier, Alan Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery
and Intelligence" had asked whether machines could think and
proposed what became known as the Turing test. Dartmouth is
where that question became a research program.

In the summer of 1956, four researchers organized a workshop
at Dartmouth College that historically marks the beginning of
AI as a field.

- **John McCarthy** (Dartmouth, later Stanford), who coined
  the term "artificial intelligence."
- **Marvin Minsky** (Harvard, later MIT).
- **Nathaniel Rochester** (IBM).
- **Claude Shannon** (Bell Labs, foundational to information
  theory).

The workshop did not produce AI agents as we understand them
today, but it established the field and its aspirations. Every
subsequent development in the space traces back to this
moment.

## The 1960s-70s: symbolic AI and early agents

Early AI research focused on symbolic reasoning, programs
that manipulated logical symbols to solve problems. Notable
work included:

- **ELIZA (1966).** Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT built the first
  chatbot, a conversational program that simulated a
  Rogerian psychotherapist. It was not intelligent, it used
  pattern matching, but it demonstrated something important
  about human interaction with conversational software.
- **SHRDLU (1970).** Terry Winograd's MIT dissertation project.
  A program that could hold a natural-language conversation
  about a blocks world, manipulating virtual objects based on
  instructions.

- **Shakey (1966-1972).** Built at the Stanford Research
  Institute (now SRI International), Shakey is credited as the
  first mobile robot that could reason about its own actions:
  it perceived its surroundings, broke a command into steps,
  planned, and recovered when a step failed. SRI keeps a
  [history of the project](https://www.sri.com/publication/artificial-intelligence-pubs/shakey-the-robot-pub/).
  If you want one machine to call the first AI agent, Shakey is
  the strongest candidate.
- **The actor model (1973).** Carl Hewitt and colleagues at MIT
  described computation as independent "actors" that send each
  other messages. That idea runs underneath much of later
  multi-agent research.

These systems are ancestors of modern agents. Shakey in
particular already had the perceive, plan, act loop that today's
agents run, just with far less capable reasoning.

## The 1980s-90s: behavior-based robotics and Rodney Brooks

Rodney Brooks at MIT changed the game with a fundamentally
different approach. His 1986 paper "A Robust Layered Control
System for a Mobile Robot" introduced the subsumption
architecture, an approach where intelligent behavior emerges
from layered simple behaviors rather than central symbolic
reasoning.

Brooks argued that intelligence is grounded in interaction with
the physical world. His MIT robots (Genghis, Herbert, and
later the Roomba, built by iRobot, the company he
co-founded) proved that agents
could be capable and useful without symbolic reasoning.

This work shaped modern thinking about agents as systems that
sense their environment and act to accomplish goals, rather
than as pure symbolic reasoners.

## The 1990s: "intelligent agents" get a definition

In 1995 Michael Wooldridge and Nicholas Jennings published
["Intelligent Agents: Theory and Practice"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/knowledge-engineering-review/article/abs/intelligent-agents-theory-and-practice/CF2A6AAEEA1DBD486EF019F6217F1597),
which gave the field its most-cited working definition: an agent
is autonomous, has social ability, is reactive to its
environment, and is pro-active about its goals. I break those
four down in
[the four core characteristics of an AI agent](/blog/types-of-ai-agents-explained).
The same decade produced belief-desire-intention (BDI) agent
architectures and the first wave of "software agents" research
for email filtering and personal assistants.


Stuart Russell (UC Berkeley) and Peter Norvig (Google) wrote
"Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach," first published in
1995. This textbook, now in its fourth edition, became the
standard
reference for AI education worldwide.

Russell and Norvig formalized the concept of an "intelligent
agent" as a system that perceives its environment through
sensors, reasons about that environment, and takes actions
through effectors to achieve goals. They defined the taxonomy
that persists in academic AI education today (simple reflex,
model-based, goal-based, utility-based, learning agents).

This textbook framework is why "AI agent" has the meaning it
has in current usage.

## The 2000s-2010s: machine learning takes over

The 2000s and 2010s saw AI move from symbolic reasoning to
statistical machine learning. Notable milestones:

- **IBM Watson (2011)** won Jeopardy! and showed what
  question-answering systems could do at scale.
- **AlphaGo (2016)** by DeepMind defeated Lee Sedol in Go,
  demonstrating reinforcement learning's power on complex
  tasks.
- **BERT (2018)** by Google showed transformer language models
  could achieve state-of-the-art on many NLP tasks.

These were not agents in the modern sense, but the techniques
they developed became foundational for what came next.

## The 2020s: LLM-based agents

The current era of AI agents began with the widespread adoption
of large language models.

- **GPT-3 (2020)** by OpenAI showed that transformer language
  models could generalize across tasks with minimal fine-tuning.
- **ChatGPT (November 2022)** brought conversational AI to
  mass consumer use.
- **AutoGPT and BabyAGI (early 2023)** were viral open-source
  demonstrations of LLM-powered agents that could pursue
  multi-step goals autonomously.
- **Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini, Meta's Llama** and
  other labs advanced the state of the art through 2023-2026.
- **Mainstream agent products (2025-2026).** OpenAI shipped
  ChatGPT agent mode in July 2025, and Google Cloud announced a
  single "Gemini agent" for work in October 2026.

The modern conception of "AI agent", a system that uses an
LLM for reasoning, has tool access, and pursues goals across
multiple steps, coalesced in this window. It was not invented
by any single company; it emerged from the combined work of
many labs and developer communities.

## The specific inventors of the pieces

If you want specific credit for the pieces of modern AI agent
architecture:

- **Chain-of-thought reasoning** was popularized by Jason Wei
  and colleagues at Google Research in the 2022 paper "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language
  Models."
- **Tool use in language models** was formalized in multiple
  papers, notably the [ReAct paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
  (Shunyu Yao et al. 2022) and Toolformer (Timo Schick et al. 2023).
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** was introduced by
  [Patrick Lewis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) at
  Facebook AI Research, UCL, and NYU in 2020.
- **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** was introduced by Anthropic in
  November 2024 as an open standard for connecting AI to tools,
  and donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in
  December 2025.
- **Agent2Agent (A2A)** protocol was launched by Google in April
  2025 for agent-to-agent communication and moved to the Linux
  Foundation that June.

Plain-English explainers of RAG and MCP are in
[RAG vs LLM vs MCP AI agents](/blog/rag-llm-mcp-ai-agent-architecture).

Each of these is a component of what makes modern agents
possible. No one of them "invented" the agent.

## The applied inventors: who shipped agents first at scale

The academic history is one thing. The commercial deployment
of AI agents in real business operations is another. Some
important early adopters:

- **Amazon Alexa (2014)** was one of the first mass-market
  voice AI products, though its capabilities in 2014 were
  narrow.
- **Google Duplex (2018)** demonstrated AI making phone calls
  on behalf of users, a controversial demo that raised
  questions about disclosure.
- **A wave of voice AI startups** including Retell, Vapi,
  Bland, and Synthflow emerged in 2022-2024, providing the
  infrastructure for the current generation of production
  voice agents.

## What is likely to be the next chapter

The history is not finished. Areas actively evolving in 2026:

- **Multi-agent systems** where specialized agents cooperate.
- **Long-horizon agents** that pursue goals across days or
  weeks rather than single conversations.
- **Embodied agents** connecting AI reasoning to physical
  robotics.
- **Agent-to-agent commerce** where agents transact on behalf
  of humans.
- **Standardized protocols** (MCP, A2A) for interoperability.

Whoever advances these will be the answer to "who invented"
in the next generation of the same question.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was the term "AI agent" first used?**
The concept traces to the 1950s, and agent research was an
active field through the 1980s. The term "intelligent agent" as
we use it today was pinned down in the mid-1990s by Wooldridge
and Jennings and popularized by Russell and Norvig's textbook.

**What was the first AI agent?**
There is no official first, but SRI's Shakey (1966-1972) is the
usual answer: the first mobile robot that perceived its world,
planned its own steps, and acted on them.

**Did OpenAI invent AI agents?**
No. OpenAI's ChatGPT and GPT models catalyzed the current
LLM-based agent era but did not invent the concept. Agents
existed in academic and commercial contexts for decades before.

**Was ELIZA an AI agent?**
Not in the modern sense. ELIZA was a chatbot using pattern
matching. It did not have goals, tools, or a decision loop.

**Who is the most influential person in modern AI agents?**
No single person. In terms of foundational academic work,
Russell and Norvig. In terms of the LLM revolution enabling
current agents, teams at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind,
Meta, and Mistral.

**Who invented agentic AI?**
Nobody owns the term either. "Agentic AI" became popular around
2023-2024 to describe LLM agents that plan and act over several
steps. The ideas underneath it are the ones in this timeline. See
[what is agentic AI in simple terms](/blog/what-is-agentic-ai).
