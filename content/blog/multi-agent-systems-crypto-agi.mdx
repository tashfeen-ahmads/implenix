<Answer q="Can AI agents talk to each other, what are crypto AI agents, and how do agentic AI and AGI connect?">
Yes, AI agents can talk to each other, through plain language, APIs, or open protocols like Google's A2A. Crypto AI agents are agents that hold a wallet and transact on a blockchain, which is different from "AI agent tokens." Three questions from the AI agent conversation that operators
keep asking me but that do not fit into deployment-focused
posts. This is the plain-English tour of multi-agent systems,
crypto AI agents, and the connection to AGI, from someone who
ships production AI voice agents and reads the research
literature but does not have a stake in the hype cycles.

## Can AI agents talk to each other?

<Answer q="Can AI agents talk to each other?">
Yes. Agents exchange messages in plain language, through APIs and message queues, or through open protocols such as Google's Agent2Agent (A2A). Inside one company this is called a multi-agent system and runs in production today. Between companies it is newer: the hard parts are identity, sharing only the right context, and keeping a human in the loop for consequential actions.
</Answer>

There are two different situations hiding in this question:
agents built by one team cooperating inside one system, and
agents from different companies meeting each other. The first is
routine. The second is where the industry is still building the
plumbing.

### What multi-agent means in practice

Instead of one AI agent handling every part of a task,
multiple specialized agents cooperate:
- A **router agent** classifies incoming requests and hands
  each to the appropriate specialist.
- **Specialist agents** each have narrower scope but deeper
  tools within that scope.
- **Coordinator agents** may synthesize outputs from multiple
  specialists.

### Real production examples

- **Complex customer service.** A router agent classifies
  incoming tickets. Billing questions go to a billing
  specialist agent with access to billing systems. Technical
  questions go to a technical specialist with access to
  product documentation. Complex cases route to humans.
- **Software engineering.** Planner agents decompose a coding
  task into subtasks. Executor agents implement each subtask.
  Reviewer agents check the output.
- **Research tasks.** A coordinator agent decomposes a research
  question. Multiple search-and-summarize agents work in
  parallel on different aspects. A synthesizer agent combines
  findings.
- **Multi-stage voice workflows.** A voice intake agent gathers
  data. A backend research agent looks up historical context.
  A response agent tailors the outbound follow-up.

### Where multi-agent is overkill

For most business voice AI deployments, a well-scoped single
agent is enough. Multi-agent architectures are worth the
coordination overhead only when the task genuinely decomposes
into specialized subtasks that benefit from separate context.

### How agents actually communicate

- **Plain language.** One agent sends another a message in
  English, the same way it would talk to a person. Flexible, but
  easy to misread.
- **Structured messages.** Agents pass JSON over APIs, queues, or
  events. Less flexible, much more reliable. Most production
  systems use this.
- **An orchestrator.** A coordinating program or agent decides
  who works on what and passes results along.
- **Open protocols.** Shared standards so agents built by
  different vendors can find each other and hand off work.

### A2A and MCP: the two protocols to know

Google launched the Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol in April 2025 so
agents from different vendors could discover each other, delegate
tasks, and return results. In June 2025 it
[moved to the Linux Foundation](https://sdtimes.com/ai/googles-agent2agent-protocol-finds-new-home-at-the-linux-foundation/)
with backers including AWS, Cisco, Microsoft, Salesforce, SAP, and
ServiceNow. In A2A, each agent publishes a description of what it
can do, and other agents send it tasks.

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) solves a neighboring problem: it
connects an agent to tools and data, not to other agents. A2A is
agent-to-agent; MCP is agent-to-tool. Many systems use both. I
explain MCP in
[RAG vs LLM vs MCP AI agents](/blog/rag-llm-mcp-ai-agent-architecture).

### What goes wrong when agents talk

Generating the message is the easy part. The hard parts:
- **Identity.** Is the agent on the other end who it says it is?
- **Context leakage.** Agents should share only what the other
  side needs, not the whole customer record.
- **Loops and drift.** Two agents can bounce a task back and
  forth, or each assume the other checked something.
- **Accountability.** Someone needs a log of who decided what.

### When an AI agent calls your AI receptionist

This is not hypothetical for local businesses. Google Search now
has AI that phones businesses to ask about pricing and
availability on a shopper's behalf. When that call reaches an AI
receptionist, two agents are talking to each other in plain
spoken English. A well-built receptionist treats it like any
other caller: answers the question, offers to book, and does not
share anything it would not tell a person. If it goes to
voicemail, you are left out of the comparison. See
[how an AI receptionist works](/how-does-an-ai-receptionist-work)
for the call flow.

## What are AI agents in crypto?

<Answer q="What are AI agents in crypto?">
Crypto AI agents are AI programs that control a crypto wallet and can read blockchain data, make decisions, and send transactions on their own, such as trading, paying for services, or voting in a DAO. They are different from "AI agent tokens," which are coins that trade on the hype around agents. Real experiments exist; proven, durable use cases are still few.
</Answer>

The phrase "AI agents crypto" gets used for two different things,
and mixing them up is how people lose money.

- **AI agents that use crypto.** Software that holds a wallet and
  transacts. This is the technical category.
- **AI agent tokens.** Coins issued by or around agent projects,
  which trade on speculation. Owning the token does not give you
  an agent, and the token price says little about whether the
  agent does anything useful.

Names you will run into: Truth Terminal, an AI persona on X whose
posts sparked a memecoin craze in 2024; ElizaOS (formerly ai16z),
an open-source framework for building crypto agents; and Virtuals
Protocol, a platform for launching tokenized agents. On the
infrastructure side, Coinbase launched
[x402](https://www.theblock.co/learn/391983/what-is-coinbases-x402-protocol)
in May 2025, an open standard that lets agents pay for APIs and
web content with stablecoins over ordinary web requests.

### The general pattern

A crypto AI agent:
- Has a wallet with real assets under its control (typically
  through a smart contract that grants it permissions).
- Reads on-chain data and off-chain signals.
- Executes transactions autonomously, swaps, transfers, DAO
  votes, contract interactions.
- Operates according to programmed rules and often LLM-driven
  reasoning.

### What they are used for today

- **Autonomous trading strategies.** Agent monitors market
  conditions and executes trades within programmed
  constraints.
- **DAO governance participation.** Agent reads proposals and
  votes according to preferences it was configured with.
- **Yield farming automation.** Moving assets between DeFi
  protocols based on yield conditions.
- **Micropayment coordination.** Handling small payments for
  services rendered agent-to-agent.
- **Autonomous business operations** as experiments, agents
  that manage their own operating expenses in crypto.

### Where the honest skepticism belongs

- Most current use cases either replicate what algorithmic
  trading has done for years or are speculative demos.
- The "agent that runs its own business" narratives are
  interesting research but rarely produce sustained economic
  value.
- Regulatory ambiguity around autonomous financial agents is
  significant.
- The intersection of AI hype and crypto hype tends to produce
  more marketing than substance.

There is genuine research and development here. There is also
a lot of noise. Distinguish carefully.

## How does agentic AI connect to AGI?

<Answer q="How are agentic AI and AGI connected?">
Agentic AI is AI that pursues goals by planning and taking actions within a narrow scope. AGI is a hypothetical system with human-level ability across almost any task. They connect through autonomy: researchers treat how independently a system acts as one axis of progress toward AGI. Agentic AI is a step on that path, not the destination.
</Answer>

Agentic AI is a stepping stone. It is not the same thing. If you
want the basics first, read
[what agentic AI is in simple terms](/blog/what-is-agentic-ai).

### What AGI means (approximately)

AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) is hypothetically a
system with human-level general reasoning across all domains.
Not a chatbot that is good at some tasks, a system that can
learn and reason about arbitrary new problems the way humans
can. No such system exists today, there is no single agreed
definition, and the timeline to it is contested even among
leading researchers.

The most useful framework I have found is Google DeepMind's
["Levels of AGI"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462) paper
(Morris et al., 2023). It rates systems on how well they perform
and how general they are, and treats **autonomy** as a separate
scale, from a tool a human drives to a fully autonomous agent.
That separation is the clearest way to see the connection: you
can have a highly autonomous system that is narrow (today's
agents) or a broadly capable system that only acts when asked
(today's chat assistants). AGI would need high capability,
breadth, and the judgment to use autonomy safely.

### What agentic AI is

Narrow systems that pursue defined goals within specific
scopes, using tools and reasoning to accomplish tasks. My
voice agent that books your appointment is agentic AI. Also
narrow.

### The research connection

The techniques used in production agentic AI (chain-of-thought
reasoning, tool use, retrieval augmentation, multi-agent
coordination) are informed by AGI research and vice versa.
Progress on scalable agentic architectures is one axis on
which AGI research advances.

But the leap from "AI that books dental appointments" to
"AI with human-level general reasoning" is enormous and
probably not just a matter of scaling up what we have today.

### Where the connection gets overstated

- "Agentic AI is AGI", no, agentic AI is a category of
  narrow AI systems.
- "Every agentic system is a step toward AGI", no, most
  production agentic AI is targeted engineering, not AGI
  research.
- "Deploying agentic AI in your business means you're on the
  path to AGI", no, you are automating specific business
  workflows.

### Where the connection is real

- **Multi-agent coordination.** How to get multiple agents to
  cooperate effectively is genuinely relevant to AGI
  scenarios.
- **Long-horizon planning.** Agents that pursue goals across
  time is a real research direction with AGI relevance.
- **Grounded reasoning.** How AI systems reason accurately
  about the world is core to both.

## The frontier questions that get less attention

Beyond the three headline questions, some others operators
should be aware of:

- **Agent alignment and control.** How do we ensure agents
  pursue the goals we intended? Growing area of research and
  practical concern.
- **Agent identity and authentication.** How do systems verify
  that an incoming request is from a legitimate agent versus a
  malicious one?
- **Agent liability.** When an agent takes an action that
  causes harm, who is responsible? Legal frameworks are
  incomplete.
- **Environmental cost.** Compute-intensive agents at scale
  have real energy implications. This is a real consideration
  for deployment scale decisions.

## What all of this means for a business operator today

Most of this is not immediately relevant to shipping a voice
AI on your inbound line. Multi-agent architectures matter if
you have complex support workflows. Crypto AI agents are
probably not relevant to your business unless you are
specifically in that space. AGI is a research topic, not a
product decision.

The practical takeaway: understand these terms well enough to
recognize when vendors are using them appropriately versus for
marketing hype. Do not let frontier concepts distract from
scoping your specific business deployment correctly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I build a multi-agent system for my small business?**
Probably not worth it. Small business use cases usually fit a
well-scoped single agent. Multi-agent overhead is not
justified below meaningful complexity.

**Are crypto AI agents legal?**
Legally uncertain in many jurisdictions. Consult counsel
before deploying agents that hold or transact in real assets
on your behalf.

**Is AGI coming soon?**
Depends who you ask. Timelines from serious researchers range
from a decade to indefinite. Building your business plan
around specific AGI timelines is probably unwise.

**What is the difference between agentic AI and generative
AI?**
Generative AI produces content when prompted. Agentic AI takes
actions to accomplish goals. Agentic systems typically use
generative models under the hood.

**Should I care about A2A protocol or MCP interoperability?**
Only if you are building complex multi-vendor agent
architectures. For most business deployments, direct API
integration works fine.

**Can two AI voice agents have a phone conversation?**
Yes. It already happens when an AI assistant calls a business
answered by an AI receptionist. Both sides speak normal language,
so the call works like any other, as long as each side sticks to
its own scope.

**Are AI agent crypto tokens a good investment?**
I do not give investment advice, and this article is not
financial advice. The honest observation is that agent tokens are
priced on attention, not on what the agent earns, and that is a
risky foundation.

**Is ChatGPT AGI?**
No. ChatGPT and similar assistants are capable and general in
some ways, and they now have agent modes, but they still fail at
many tasks humans do easily and do not meet any widely accepted
definition of AGI.

## Where to go from here

If you are running a business and evaluating AI, focus on the
narrow, well-scoped deployments that produce measurable value
today. The frontier stuff is interesting but rarely the
right first investment. Multi-agent architectures, crypto
agents, and AGI-adjacent capabilities are worth watching but
not worth betting operational infrastructure on.
