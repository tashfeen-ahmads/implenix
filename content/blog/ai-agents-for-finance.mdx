<Answer q="What do AI agents do in financial services?">
AI voice agents in financial services handle inbound lead intake for mortgage brokers, RIAs, and insurance agencies, capturing the loan scenario, coverage need, or advisory situation, running licensing-and-jurisdiction gates, and booking discovery calls with a licensed representative. They do not quote rates, recommend products, or offer any advice that would require a license (Series 6/7/63/65/66, insurance license, NMLS). The scope is intake and booking, always ending with a licensed human.
</Answer>

Financial services is the category with the highest compliance
gravity of anything I ship. Mortgage brokers, registered
investment advisors, and insurance agencies all live inside
regulatory frameworks (NMLS, FINRA, state insurance departments,
SEC for RIAs above a size threshold) that shape what a voice
agent may say on a phone call. Getting the scope right is not
optional. Getting it wrong means the same enforcement letters a
human employee would trigger. This is a guide for the operator
evaluating a voice agent in financial services, from someone
who has actually deployed them under those constraints.

## The financial services call problem

The specifics differ by segment but the shape is similar.

- **Mortgage brokers** get a flood of inbound rate-shoppers and
  refinance inquiries, often at hours the loan officer is off.
  Time-to-first-touch is the differentiator.
- **RIAs and financial advisors** get referral-and-web-lead
  inbound that needs qualification before it burns the advisor's
  discovery-call calendar. The compliance shell around the
  conversation matters.
- **Insurance agencies** get quote-shopping inbound at scale.
  Auto and home carriers reward speed; whoever quotes first often
  binds first.

Across the segment, the common thread is that inbound is
high-value, latency-sensitive, and regulatory-adjacent. Voice AI
sits well in this gap as long as scope is disciplined.

## What an AI agent actually does for a mortgage broker

- **Answer the inbound refinance/purchase call.** Greets, asks
  purchase-or-refi, captures loan amount, property state, and
  approximate credit tier.
- **Run a licensing gate.** Broker licensed in every state? If not,
  the agent checks the state against the licensing list before
  booking. Out-of-state calls get politely referred elsewhere
  rather than converted into an ineligible lead.
- **Capture the scenario at a high level.** Loan amount, purchase
  price or estimated home value, employment status
  (W-2/self-employed), whether the borrower has a real estate
  contract yet.
- **Book onto the loan officer's discovery calendar.** Fifteen or
  thirty-minute intake with the licensed originator.
- **Handle the "what is your rate" question correctly.** The agent
  does not quote a rate. Ever. It explains that rates are
  scenario-specific and books the discovery call.

## What an AI agent does for an RIA or financial advisor

- **Inbound referral intake.** Captures how the caller heard about
  the firm, general life-stage context (retirement planning,
  business sale, inheritance, divorce), and asset ballpark.
- **Qualification for the discovery call.** RIAs typically screen
  for minimum investable assets. The agent handles the
  screen politely, with a warm decline for below-minimum callers
  and an optional referral to a lower-minimum affiliate if
  arranged.
- **Booking on the advisor's discovery calendar.**
- **Handles no-shows and reschedules on a two-touch cadence.**

The agent never recommends a product, discusses fees on assets
under management in specific dollar terms, or opines on any
investment strategy. The RIA's compliance officer signs off on the
greeting language before deployment.

## What an AI agent does for an insurance agency

- **Inbound quote intake.** Auto, home, life, commercial, captures
  the line of coverage, ZIP, drivers or dwelling data, current
  carrier if renewing.
- **Books a quote callback with a licensed producer.**
- **For simple auto quotes at agencies with rating APIs**, some
  deployments have the agent trigger a preliminary rate
  indication. This is the exception, not the default, and it is
  scoped tightly.
- **Handles claim reporting** by capturing the claim details and
  routing to the carrier claims line or the agency's claim intake
  workflow.

## The compliance layer I insist on

Every financial services deployment gets a "hard stop" list that
includes at minimum:

- No rate quotes on mortgage, insurance, or investment products.
- No recommendation of specific products.
- No discussion of returns, historical or projected.
- No claim of licensed status. The agent identifies as a virtual
  intake assistant, not as a loan officer, advisor, or producer.
- No solicitation activity that would require the agent's own
  licensing (relevant primarily for insurance where the "solicit"
  definition is broad).
- Prompt disclosure that the caller is speaking with an AI.

For NMLS-regulated activity specifically, I flag any deployment
where the agent's behavior could be characterized as "taking a
mortgage application" and route away from that scope. Application
intake is loan-originator work. The agent handles pre-application
intake and booking.

For RIAs, the greeting language is written jointly with the CCO
and reviewed against Form ADV disclosures. I do not skip that
review.

For insurance, licensing questions vary by state. Some state
insurance departments have taken positions on AI intake. I check
the operator's state before scope-setting.

For mortgage, the LOS (Encompass, LendingPad, Byte) integrations
are heavier lifts than most CRMs. Some brokers run a lightweight
CRM (Shape, HubSpot) upstream of the LOS and I integrate at the
CRM layer with LOS-touch happening downstream by the LO. This is
cleaner and I recommend it in most cases.

For RIA, Redtail and Wealthbox both have workable APIs. Salesforce
Financial Services Cloud is the enterprise option and its
integration surface is deep but longer to deploy.

For insurance, AMS providers vary. EZLynx has a workable
integration path. Applied Epic is the enterprise standard and its
API access is agency-tier-gated.

## Pricing and payback

Financial services deployments run $1,100 to $2,200 per month all
in, higher than most verticals because of the compliance layer and
the deeper integrations.

Payback math varies wildly by segment.

- **Mortgage:** Average commission per closed loan runs $2,500 to
  $5,000. Recovering 2 to 4 loans per year that would have gone
  to voicemail typically covers the agent's annual cost within
  the first month.
- **RIA:** One qualified discovery call that converts to a
  managed account of $500K+ generates decades of AUM revenue. The
  math is trivially positive if the agent surfaces even one such
  lead per year.
- **Insurance:** Depends heavily on line and commission structure.
  Personal lines P&C typically break even in 60 to 90 days.

## Compliance considerations I would not skip

1. **Licensing gates.** Every deployment includes a state-by-state
   licensing check before any booking activity.
2. **Recording consent.** The greeting carries an audible
   notification everywhere, two-party consent states require it
   and one-party states benefit from the same transparency.
3. **TCPA on outbound.** Follow-up and reactivation sequences to
   mobile numbers require prior express written consent. This is a
   real enforcement area with real settlements.
4. **State-specific bar-equivalent opinions.** For insurance and
   mortgage, check the operator's state regulator for any published
   position on AI intake.
5. **Data retention.** Financial records have specific retention
   requirements (typically 3 to 7 years depending on segment).
   Call recordings covering intake conversations get flagged and
   retained accordingly.

## What the deployment looks like

Financial services deployments run 14 to 28 business days, the
longest of any vertical I ship, primarily because of the
compliance review cycles.

- **Days 1-5:** Discovery. Call log review, "hard stop" list,
  compliance officer briefing.
- **Days 6-12:** Script build with compliance in the loop.
- **Days 13-20:** Integration wiring, licensing gate configuration,
  transcript storage under access controls.
- **Days 21-25:** Test calls. Mock compliance calls including
  callers pushing for rate quotes, callers asking for
  recommendations, callers with out-of-state properties.
- **Days 26-28:** Cutover with a 72-hour silent monitor.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the agent take a mortgage application?**
No. Application intake is loan-originator work under NMLS. The
agent runs pre-application intake and books the discovery call
with the licensed originator.

**Can the agent quote insurance rates?**
Generally no. Some P&C carriers expose rating APIs that could
support preliminary indications, and a handful of my deployments
use them, but only with the carrier's and the state regulator's
positions confirmed in advance.

**What about robocall regulations?**
Inbound calls the caller placed are unrestricted. Outbound
follow-up to a mobile requires prior express written consent
under TCPA, and the agent handles the STOP flow correctly.

**How do you handle callers who ask what the agent thinks about
their situation?**
The agent declines cleanly: "That is exactly what the loan
officer / advisor / producer will walk through in the discovery
call. I do not want to give you information that would not apply
to your specific situation." Callers accept this.

**What if a caller reports a claim?**
Insurance deployments include claim reporting intake. The agent
captures the details and routes to the carrier claims line or the
agency's claim intake process depending on setup.

## When a financial services agent is the wrong fit

- Single-producer shops doing under 30 inbound calls per month.
- Firms without a designated compliance officer or, for RIAs,
  without a Form ADV update capacity when the greeting language
  needs review.
- Operators philosophically opposed to any pre-licensed-human
  intake automation. This is a fit conversation, not a technical
  one.
