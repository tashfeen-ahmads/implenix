<Answer q="What does an AI agent do for a med spa?">
An AI agent for a medspa answers inbound booking calls 24/7, matches the caller's requested service (Botox, filler, laser hair removal, chemical peels, membership consultations) to the correct provider and treatment duration, checks the practice management system for real availability, and books directly. It handles package renewals and post-treatment follow-ups. It does not give medical advice, does not price treatments over the phone without disclaimers, and it treats every call as potential PHI.
</Answer>

Medspa inbound has a specific structural problem. Your best client
call the practice at 8pm on a Wednesday to book their next Botox
appointment, and your provider is either mid-treatment or off for
the night. That one missed call is a booked appointment lost to the
medspa down the street with a real-time booking widget. I have
shipped voice agents into aesthetic practices in Miami, Los Angeles,
Dallas, and three suburbs of Chicago. The pattern that pays for the
deployment is the same everywhere, after-hours booking capture and
service-specific intake, not any of the flashy stuff.

## The medspa call log I usually see

- Inbound answer rate during business hours: 60 to 75 percent
  because the front desk is running check-ins and check-outs.
- After-hours answer rate: near zero unless the practice pays for
  a general answering service, which handles medspa calls poorly
  because they do not know the service catalog.
- Average time-to-callback on a voicemail booking request: 3 to
  9 hours the following business day.
- Share of after-hours booking requests that never convert: 30 to
  45 percent. The caller books somewhere else.
- New-client vs existing-client mix on inbound: about 30 percent
  new consultations, 70 percent existing-client bookings and
  package questions.

## What an AI agent actually does for a medspa

- **Answer every inbound within one ring.** The greeting matches
  the practice voice, warm, aesthetic-industry appropriate, no
  medical-office coldness.
- **Match the caller's service request to the practice catalog.**
  This is the highest-value behavior. Callers say "I want lip
  filler" or "the injectable that lasts longer than Botox" or "the
  one Kim K uses." The agent maps loose language to the catalog
  and confirms.
- **Route to the right provider.** Injector-only services go to
  the injector's calendar. Aesthetician services go to the
  aesthetician. Consultations for surgical referral go to the
  physician's consult calendar.
- **Check real availability.** Direct read against the practice
  management system (Boulevard, Zenoti, Vagaro, Mindbody, Aesthetic
  Record), not a synced snapshot. Real-time so double-booking
  cannot happen.
- **Book, take deposit if required.** Many medspas require a
  deposit to hold high-demand slots. The agent triggers a Stripe
  or Square Terminal link over SMS.
- **Handle package inquiries.** Existing clients ask "how many
  sessions do I have left on my laser package?" The agent looks up
  and answers.
- **Post-treatment follow-up.** Two-touch sequence 48 hours and
  10 days after treatment, check-in on comfort, book the next
  session or the package renewal.

## What I explicitly do not build into a medspa agent

- Medical advice about candidacy. "Am I a good candidate for
  laser hair removal on darker skin?" is an in-person conversation
  with a licensed provider.
- Contraindication screening. The agent can note "you mentioned
  pregnancy, the provider will confirm during your visit" but
  does not clear or reject on the phone.
- Off-label discussion of any product.
- Pricing on treatments where the practice does not publish list
  prices. The agent references a range if the practice publishes
  one, otherwise it books a consultation.

Boulevard is the fastest integration path in medspa today. Its API
surface is well-documented and its booking model matches how
aesthetic practices actually run. Zenoti is more established at
larger multi-location chains. Aesthetic Record is the PHI-first
option and is the right choice for practices with a physician-led
model or heavier procedural work. All three ship in production for
me.

## The HIPAA layer, which is non-negotiable

Medspa calls contain PHI. Sometimes obvious PHI ("I have a rash"),
sometimes ambient PHI (the fact that this caller is a Botox client
is itself protected). I treat every call as PHI from the moment
the phone rings.

- **BAA required.** With the voice model vendor, with the
  telephony vendor, with the transcript storage layer. Everything
  in the pipeline. If a vendor cannot sign a BAA, they are not in
  the deployment.
- **Recording opt-in and greeting-level disclosure.** The agent
  discloses the recording in the opening beat regardless of state
  requirement.
- **Transcript storage.** Access-controlled, encrypted at rest,
  retention policy that matches the practice's own record
  retention (typically 7 years for medical records but medspa
  varies by state).
- **Minimum necessary.** The agent captures what is needed for
  booking. It does not solicit medical history over the phone.
  That happens in-person on the intake form.
- **Breach protocol.** Written incident response plan for any
  disclosure event. This is a legal formality that becomes
  critical if you ever need it.

I decline medspa deployments where the operator does not want the
BAA layer. That work is not worth the risk.

## The package retention play

Medspa economics run on packages. Six sessions of laser, quarterly
Botox memberships, monthly facial subscriptions. The lifetime
value of a client is dictated by whether they renew. The
retention play is where the agent's ROI moves from "good" to
"outsized."

The sequence:
- **Day 3 post-treatment:** Comfort check-in SMS. If comfort issue
  is flagged, escalate to provider.
- **Day 10:** Second SMS with an offer to book the next session
  in the package.
- **Two weeks before package expiration:** Voice call to offer
  renewal, often with a package-renewal price incentive if the
  practice publishes one.

On medspa deployments I track, this sequence improves package
renewal rates by 12 to 22 percentage points. For a practice doing
$180,000 per year in package revenue, moving renewal from 55 to
70 percent is roughly $27,000 in retained revenue.

## What the deployment looks like

Medspa deployments go live in 72 hours from kickoff.

- **Hours 0-24:** Discovery and script draft. Listen to recent
  calls, walk the service catalog with the practice manager,
  capture the "hard stop" list around medical advice, then map
  services to the catalog, provider routing, deposit handling,
  and package-inquiry logic.
- **Hours 24-48:** PMS integration, calendar wiring, deposit
  webhook, transcript storage under BAA-covered infrastructure.
- **Hours 48-72:** Test calls. Full battery including callers
  with unusual service requests, callers with contraindications,
  callers asking for pricing on treatments not publicly listed.
  Your sign-off, then go-live.
- **After go-live:** 48-hour silent monitor.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this HIPAA compliant?**
Yes, with the right infrastructure. BAAs across the vendor stack,
access-controlled transcript storage, minimum-necessary intake,
and a written breach protocol. I do not ship without those.

**What if a client asks about pricing?**
If the practice publishes a range, the agent shares the range and
notes final pricing depends on the consultation. If the practice
does not publish, the agent books a complimentary consultation
and defers pricing to the provider.

**Can the agent take payment for the deposit?**
Yes, over SMS via a Stripe or Square terminal link. Not by voice, card-not-present PCI compliance for voice is a headache I decline
to inherit.

**What about existing clients calling for medication questions or
adverse reaction concerns?**
Immediate transfer to the on-call provider. The agent does not
attempt to triage medical concerns.

**How does it handle new consultation bookings?**
Captures the service of interest, prior treatment history at a
high level, and any specific provider preference, then books the
consultation on the appropriate provider's calendar.

## Where a medspa agent is the wrong fit

- Solo aesthetician doing under 100 bookings per month.
- Practices where the owner personally screens every new-client
  call for aesthetic and cultural fit before booking.
- Practices with a service catalog that changes weekly and no
  centralized way to update the agent's knowledge base.
