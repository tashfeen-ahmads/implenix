<Answer q="What is an HVAC AI agent?">
An HVAC AI agent is a voice AI that answers your inbound service line 24/7, triages emergency calls from routine ones, captures address, system type, and symptom, then either books the job against your dispatch calendar or hands the caller to your on-call technician. In HVAC specifically, the emergency triage layer is where the value lives, a "no heat" call at 11pm on a Sunday cannot wait for voicemail.
</Answer>

I have shipped AI voice agents into HVAC shops in Texas, the
Northeast, the Pacific Northwest, and one very cold county in the
UP of Michigan. The pattern is the same everywhere: HVAC inbound is
the highest-signal, highest-loss phone line I have ever measured.
Every missed call is either an emergency dispatch that walks to a
competitor within twelve minutes, or a routine service booking that
never gets rescheduled. This is a guide for the operator who wants
to understand what an HVAC AI agent actually does on a real service
call, not the pitch deck version.

## The situation most HVAC owners walk in with

Here is the call log I usually see in the first discovery session.
It is not exceptional. It is the median.

- Inbound answer rate during business hours: 58 to 71 percent.
- Inbound answer rate after 5pm: under 30 percent, often near zero.
- Average time to callback on a voicemail: 47 minutes.
- Estimated share of after-hours voicemails that never got returned
  the next morning: about one in three.
- Percentage of missed calls that showed up as a booked job later:
  about 8 percent.

That last number is the one that stings. When you miss an HVAC
call, you almost never win it back. Homeowners with a broken
furnace do not sit on their hands. They dial the next Google result.
Emergency-service search intent has a shelf life measured in
minutes, and after 6pm on a weekend it is measured in seconds.

## What an HVAC AI agent actually does

I want to be direct about scope. A voice AI is not a magic dispatch
platform. It is a phone agent, a voice on the other end of a call.
What it can do reliably today, in production:

- **Answer every inbound within one ring.** No IVR tree. No "press
  1 for service." The agent picks up and greets the caller by your
  business name in a natural voice.
- **Distinguish emergency from routine.** This is the highest-value
  behavior in HVAC. "No heat," "no AC in summer," "smell of gas,"
  "water leaking from the unit," "carbon monoxide alarm going off"
these get flagged and routed differently than "I want to
  schedule my spring tune-up."
- **Capture the full intake.** Service address, phone number, unit
  type (furnace, AC, boiler, mini-split, heat pump), approximate
  age of system, and a plain-language symptom description.
- **Book against your live dispatch calendar.** This works with
  ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, and FieldEdge in production.
  The agent reads real technician availability and books directly.
- **Warm-transfer the emergencies.** For dispatch-eligible calls,
  the agent can bridge the caller to your on-call tech, or SMS the
  tech with the intake and stay on the line until pickup.
- **Follow up on quotes.** After a tech gives an estimate, the
  agent runs a four-touch follow-up sequence (call, SMS, call, SMS)
  spaced over ten days. This alone typically recovers 15 to 22
  percent of unclosed quotes.

What it cannot do, and I want operators to hear this clearly: it
does not diagnose HVAC systems, it does not price jobs on the
phone, and it does not replace judgment on a genuinely ambiguous
service situation. It answers, triages, and books. That is enough.

## Where an HVAC AI agent handles well and where a human still wins

I get asked this in every sales call, so here is the honest split.

### Handles well

- After-hours emergency triage and warm-transfer to on-call.
- Routine service booking, spring and fall tune-ups, annual
  contract renewals.
- New-customer intake calls where the caller mostly wants a quote
  and a callback.
- Quote follow-up sequences on unclosed estimates.
- Reminder calls the day before a scheduled visit.

### A human still wins

- Any call where the customer is upset about a prior job. The
  agent will route this to your service manager immediately, and it
  should, no automation belongs in a repair-complaint escalation.
- Commercial accounts where the contact wants to discuss ongoing
  maintenance contracts, warranty terms, or multi-site rollouts.
- Any diagnostic conversation. Homeowners will occasionally try to
  describe symptoms and get advice on the phone. The right answer
  is always "let a tech look at it," and a human delivers that with
  more warmth.

I have seen shops try to push the agent into diagnostic
conversations. It goes badly. Voice AI is not the right layer for
troubleshooting a rooftop unit over the phone with a nervous
homeowner. Keep the scope tight.

## How the integrations actually work

The two integrations that determine whether an HVAC deployment
succeeds or fails are the dispatch calendar and the CRM.

ServiceTitan is the most common request. It has the deepest API
surface but also the most quirks around timezones and technician
capacity rules. Housecall Pro is faster to integrate but the
dispatch calendar layer requires slightly more logic on our side.
Jobber has the cleanest developer experience and I recommend it
without hesitation for shops under twenty techs.

For SMS the standard is Twilio. For payment on quote acceptance,
Stripe or the CRM's native invoice link. For call recording, we
use signed S3 URLs with a 30-day retention default. All recordings
are opt-in and the agent announces the recording at call open per
state law, a two-party consent state like California, Florida, or
Massachusetts requires this and I set it as default everywhere.

## What a real HVAC deployment looks like week by week

Standard timeline is 7 to 14 business days. Here is what those
days actually contain.

- **Days 1-2:** Discovery. We listen to 20 to 40 of your recent
  inbound calls and write the intake logic against your actual
  vocabulary, "no heat" versus "furnace not working" versus "cold
  house." Your language is what the agent uses.
- **Days 3-5:** Script build. Emergency branches, dispatch rules,
  transfer conditions, quote-follow-up cadence. This is the phase
  where the operator's judgment matters most.
- **Days 6-8:** Integration wiring. Calendar reads, job creation,
  CRM writeback, SMS to on-call, warm-transfer bridge.
- **Days 9-10:** Test calls. We run 30 to 50 scripted and freeform
  calls against a shadow number. Owner listens to every one.
- **Days 11-14:** Cutover. Port forwards, or full port of the main
  number. We run in "silent monitor" mode for 48 hours so a human
  is always available to catch a bad transfer.

The failure modes I have seen come almost entirely from skipping
the discovery week. If the agent does not speak your callers'
vocabulary, it feels wrong on the first call. Do not skip that
step.

## The ROI math no one publishes

I dislike blanket claims like "3x more bookings." The real math is
per-shop and depends on your inbound volume, average job value, and
current answer rate. Here is the framework I use.

<Pull>
Missed calls per month × average booked-job value × current
close-rate on answered calls = current monthly loss from missed
inbound.
</Pull>

For a shop doing 800 inbound calls per month, missing 30 percent
of them, with an average booked-job value of $340 and a 55 percent
close rate on answered calls, the loss looks like this:

- 800 × 0.30 = 240 missed calls.
- 240 × $340 × 0.55 = **$44,880 per month in unbooked pipeline**.

Not every missed call becomes a booked job with an AI agent, of
course. Realistic capture is 65 to 80 percent of previously-missed
calls booked or triaged, the rest are wrong numbers, spam, and
callers who hung up before intent was clear. Applying a 70 percent
capture rate: **$31,416 per month in newly-booked revenue** on a
$1,400 to $2,200 per month agent bill.

This is why HVAC has the fastest payback of any vertical we ship.
Median payback in HVAC deployments we track internally sits
between 8 and 14 days. If yours takes longer, the discovery week
was skipped or the emergency branch is not tuned correctly.

## Costs, honestly

There are three cost layers to plan for.

1. **Build fee.** One-time. Ranges $2,500 to $8,000 depending on
   how many locations, how many CRMs, and whether ServiceTitan is
   involved (it usually adds a few days).
2. **Monthly platform + telephony.** Voice minutes, SMS, hosting,
   telephony carrier fees. For a shop under 2,000 inbound minutes
   per month, budget $700 to $1,400. Above 5,000 minutes, negotiate
   volume rates.
3. **Tuning retainer.** Optional. Some operators want us to review
   flagged calls monthly and adjust scripts. Budget $400 to $900
   per month for this.

There is no version of a real deployment that comes in under $600
per month all-in, and I am suspicious of any vendor quoting that
number. What that price actually buys is a template voice bot that
did not go through the discovery week.

## Compliance notes I would not skip

- Two-party consent recording states require the agent to announce
  the recording. Include this in the greeting, not buried later.
- If you use SMS follow-up, TCPA applies. Include an opt-out on the
  first automated SMS and honor STOP on every downstream message.
- Store call recordings and transcripts under an access-controlled
  policy. I use a 30-day default retention with the option to
  extend for specific job files.
- Do not use the agent for outbound cold calls to residential
  numbers. That is a fast path to a TCPA class action. Outbound in
  HVAC is for confirmed customers only, appointment reminders,
  quote follow-ups, membership renewals.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will callers know it is AI?**
Some will, some will not. In the deployments I have measured, about
20 to 30 percent of callers realize they are speaking to an AI at
some point in the call. The share drops for calls under 90 seconds
and rises for calls over three minutes. It is not a secret worth
protecting, an agent that pretends it is human when directly
asked will lose the caller's trust the moment they figure it out.
Tune yours to say "yes, I am a virtual assistant, and I can get a
tech to you tonight."

**What happens if the caller has an accent the agent struggles with?**
The current generation of voice models handles most North American
regional accents cleanly. Heavy accents and non-native English
speakers are where I see errors. My rule: if the agent asks the
caller to repeat themselves twice, it should automatically bridge
to the on-call tech. Do not let the agent grind on comprehension.

**How do you handle callers who ask for a specific technician?**
The intake captures the request. If that tech is on call, the
agent routes to them. If not, the agent explains who is on call
tonight and offers to leave a note for the requested tech to
follow up the next morning. Most callers accept this.

**Can it price jobs?**
No, and I would not build it to. Pricing on the phone in HVAC
requires the tech to see the unit. The agent gives a callback
promise from a real person, or a "starting at" range if the shop
publishes one.

**How is billing structured?**
Usually a monthly platform fee plus per-minute voice charges. Some
shops prefer a flat-rate bundle at a fixed monthly cost that
includes an average call volume, with overage after a threshold.
Both work. I lean toward the transparent per-minute model because
it makes the ROI math easier to defend.

## When an HVAC AI agent is the wrong answer

I try to be honest about this. If your shop does under 150 inbound
calls per month, the ROI is not there. Handle those manually. If
you are a one-tech operation where the owner answers the phone
personally and closes on the call, an AI agent will feel like
overhead. If your problem is that your techs are running late and
customers are complaining, an AI agent will not fix your dispatch
it will just make sure the complaint calls are logged.

The right fit is a shop doing 400+ inbound calls per month, with
after-hours volume of at least 25 percent, and a genuine problem
with missed emergency dispatches. That is where the agent pays for
itself in under two weeks.
