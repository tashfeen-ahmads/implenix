Security questionnaires are one of the most disliked operational
tasks in B2B software. Vendor-side teams answer the same few hundred
questions in slightly different formats, over and over. Buyer-side
security teams struggle to compare inconsistent answers. AI agents
for this workflow have become a crowded category. This is a neutral
read on the landscape from someone who is not selling a
questionnaire tool. Voice AI is my primary work.

A warning before the list: most "best AI agent for security
questionnaires" articles you will find are written by one of the
vendors, and each ranks itself first. Treat every list, including
this one, as a shortlist to test, not a verdict.

## The two sides of the questionnaire market

**Vendor side:** you sell software or services, and enterprise
prospects send you security questionnaires before buying. Your job
is to answer accurately and fast.

**Buyer side:** you buy software or services and need to assess
vendor security. Your job is to send questionnaires, evaluate the
answers, and track vendor risk.

The tools are different on each side.

## Vendor-side tools: answering questionnaires

### Vanta

Vanta's compliance platform generates questionnaire answers from the
controls and evidence it already monitors, so answers can stay
current as your controls change.

**Strengths:** grounded in real compliance data, which reduces the
"our answer says X but our controls say Y" risk.

**Limits:** works best if you are already a Vanta customer, and
answer quality depends on how mature your Vanta setup is.

### Drata and SafeBase

Drata offers questionnaire automation grounded in its own compliance
evidence. In February 2025 Drata
[announced it was acquiring SafeBase](https://drata.com/blog/acquiring-safebase),
the trust center platform. A trust center is a customer-facing page
where buyers can see your security posture, request documents, and
often skip sending a questionnaire at all.

**Strengths:** deflection (buyers self-serve) plus evidence-grounded
answers for questionnaires that still arrive.

**Limits:** the full value assumes Drata is your compliance platform.

### Conveyor

An AI-focused platform built around automating the questionnaire
workflow, with its own trust center.

**Fits:** teams that want answering and deflection from one vendor
without switching compliance platforms.

### Loopio and Responsive

The long-standing answer-library tools. Both use AI to draft answers
from a maintained library and are widely used for RFPs as well as
security questionnaires.

**Strengths:** mature library management, sales tool integrations.

**Limits:** answers are only as current as the library you maintain;
they are not grounded in live compliance evidence.

### SecurityPal

Pairs AI drafting with security analysts who review and finalize
answers.

**Fits:** teams with high volume who want people, not just software,
accountable for the review.

### Newer AI-native tools

A wave of newer vendors (Wolfia, Inventive, AutoRFP, Skypher, and
others) emphasize agentic workflows: reading the questionnaire,
drafting from your documents, citing a source for every answer, and
routing anything unsupported to a human. Some are strong. All are
young. Pilot before committing.

### The vendor-side pattern that usually works

1. **A trust center** that deflects some questionnaires.
2. **An answering tool** for the ones that arrive, grounded in either
   your compliance platform or a maintained library.
3. **Human review** of every AI-drafted answer before it goes out.
   Never send AI answers without sign-off from someone qualified to
   make security representations.

## Buyer-side tools: sending and assessing questionnaires

### SecurityScorecard, Bitsight, and UpGuard

External security ratings platforms that grade vendors on publicly
observable signals. They help you triage which vendors need a full
questionnaire. UpGuard also emphasizes data-leak detection.

**Limits:** external ratings do not capture everything a
questionnaire does. They complement it.

### Whistic and other vendor-risk platforms

Platforms for running a formal third-party risk program: sending
questionnaires, collecting standard assessments, and tracking
vendors over time.

## The questionnaire formats you will see

- **SIG** from Shared Assessments, in Core and Lite versions, common
  in financial services.
- **CAIQ** from the Cloud Security Alliance, mapped to its Cloud
  Controls Matrix, common for cloud and SaaS vendors.
- **VSA** from the Vendor Security Alliance.
- **Custom questionnaires** and due diligence questionnaires.

Any serious tool should import these and export in the buyer's
format.

## How to test an AI agent for security questionnaires

The most useful advice in this category is simple: run a real
questionnaire through your shortlist and check these five things.

1. **Citations.** Does every answer link to the source document or
   control it came from? Can you click through and confirm it?
2. **What happens when the docs do not support an answer.** A good
   agent says "not found, needs review." A bad one invents a
   plausible answer.
3. **Freshness.** If you change a policy, does the next answer
   reflect it?
4. **Consistency.** Ask the same question two ways. Do you get the
   same answer?
5. **Data handling.** Where is your questionnaire content stored,
   is it used to train models, and what agreement covers it?

The citation and "not found" tests matter most, because a wrong
security answer becomes a contractual representation. The same
grounding ideas apply to any agent; I explain them in
[how RAG and LLM agents work](/blog/rag-llm-mcp-ai-agent-architecture)
and [how AI agents improve data accuracy](/blog/how-ai-agents-improve-data-accuracy).

## What AI does well here

- **Matching new questions to approved answers.** "This is the same
  as a question you answered last month," drafted for review.
- **Format translation.** The same answer reshaped for SIG, CAIQ, or
  a custom spreadsheet.
- **Consistency.** No more telling Customer A one thing and Customer
  B another.
- **Drafting from evidence.** For compliance-platform customers,
  answers pulled from live evidence.
- **Gap detection.** Flagging questions your library cannot answer.

## What AI does not do well

- **Novel, company-specific questions** that need engineering
  judgment.
- **Questions where the wording has legal consequences.**
- **Live verification.** "Is control X enabled in production right
  now?" needs someone to check.
- **Legal or compliance opinions.** "Do you comply with GDPR Article
  44?" needs counsel.

For these, AI drafts a first attempt and a qualified person verifies
or rewrites it.

## Cost and ROI: how to think about it

Pricing in this category is usually quote-based and changes often,
so get current quotes rather than trusting any published range,
including old ones in blog posts. To judge ROI, count:

- Questionnaires per year.
- Hours per questionnaire today, across sales, security, and
  engineering.
- Deals delayed while waiting on security review.

If a tool cuts a meaningful share of those hours and shortens the
review stage of your sales cycle, it pays for itself. If you only see
a handful of questionnaires a year, a well-kept answer document and a
general AI assistant with careful human review may be enough.

## The decision framework

**Vendor side:**
- A few questionnaires a year: a maintained answer document, a
  general AI assistant for drafting, and human review.
- Regular volume: a trust center plus an answering tool. Pick Vanta
  or Drata if you already use them; otherwise compare Conveyor,
  Loopio, Responsive, and SecurityPal on your own questionnaire.
- High volume across many teams: all of the above, with a named
  owner for the answer library.

**Buyer side:**
- A small vendor list: manual questionnaires and standard assessments
  (SIG, CAIQ) may be enough.
- A larger vendor list: a ratings platform for triage.
- A formal risk program: a vendor-risk platform such as Whistic or
  similar.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use ChatGPT or Claude to answer security questionnaires?**
For drafting individual answers from documents you provide, yes,
with human review and with attention to what data you paste in. For
full automation, dedicated tools add answer libraries, citations,
format handling, and audit trails.

**Is AI-generated content acceptable for security answers?**
As a draft. The final answer must be reviewed and approved by
someone qualified to make security representations.

**Do buyers know when answers were AI-generated?**
Often not, and many do not mind as long as the answers are accurate.
Some buyers now ask directly. Answer honestly.

**Do these tools handle SIG, CAIQ, and VSA?**
The major tools support the standard formats plus custom
spreadsheets. Confirm with a real file during your trial.

**Is my questionnaire content kept private?**
Reputable vendors treat it as confidential under a data agreement.
Ask whether your content is used for model training and get the
answer in writing.

## Where to go from here

On the vendor side, start by checking whether your compliance
platform already includes questionnaire AI, then test one or two
answering tools on a real recent questionnaire using the five checks
above. On the buyer side, start with a ratings platform for triage.
