I have shipped voice agents to just past a hundred small
businesses. The question I get most often before any of them is
"which one is the best?" That question does not have a single
answer, and any vendor giving you one is selling. What actually
matters is matching the tier of solution to the tier of your
operation. This piece is a decision framework, not a
recommendation list.

## The three tiers of voice AI for small business

The market has settled into three practical tiers. Each one fits
a different type of operator.

### Tier 1: DIY templated bots ($50 to $200 per month)

Think of these as SaaS voice bots. You sign up, follow a wizard,
paste in your business info, and it goes live. Vendors in this
tier include Goodcall, some of the CallJoy successors, and a
long tail of self-serve platforms.

**Who this fits:** Solo operators, single-location shops doing
under 200 inbound calls per month with straightforward booking
needs. A dog groomer, a small law office, a solo real estate
agent, a boutique fitness studio.

**Strengths:**
- Cheap and fast to deploy.
- No professional services engagement.
- Adequate for pure appointment-booking flow.

**Limits:**
- Integration surface is shallow. If you run something outside
  Google Calendar or a couple of common CRMs, you are stuck.
- Voice quality is usually a step below the higher tiers.
- Scripting is templated. You cannot tune for industry-specific
  vocabulary the way discovery-led deployments do.
- Escalation and transfer logic is limited.

### Tier 2: Platform with agency layer ($500 to $1,500 per month)

Platforms like Retell, Vapi, and Bland provide the infrastructure;
an agency does the discovery, scripting,
integration, and ongoing tuning on top. This is where most of the
small-business market sits.

**Who this fits:** Multi-location small businesses, service
operators doing 200 to 1,000 inbound calls per month, any shop
with real integration needs (ServiceTitan, Boulevard, Clio,
Follow Up Boss).

**Strengths:**
- Deep integration wiring possible.
- Industry-specific script tuning.
- Ongoing optimization from a human doing QA on flagged calls.
- Escalation logic and transfer rules built to your operation.

**Limits:**
- More expensive than tier 1.
- Requires real discovery work to deploy correctly.
- Lock-in to the agency's platform relationship.

### Tier 3: Fully custom deployment ($1,000 to $2,500 per month)

Custom-built voice agents running on infrastructure the agency
owns, or on the platform layer with heavy custom code around it.
This is what you want if you have unusual requirements, multi-lingual callers, complex custom-built ERP integration, industry
compliance layers.

**Who this fits:** Large SMBs, multi-location chains doing 1,000+
inbound calls per month, operations with unique integration
surface or compliance requirements (medspa with Aesthetic Record,
enterprise legal with Filevine, credit unions).

**Strengths:**
- Any integration, any workflow.
- Deep compliance customization (HIPAA, GLBA, industry-specific).
- Custom voice cloning for brand consistency.
- Full ownership of the deployment IP.

**Limits:**
- Highest cost.
- Longest deployment timeline.
- More overhead to manage.

## How to know which tier fits

Answer these five questions honestly and the tier picks itself.

1. **How many inbound calls per month do you actually handle?**
   Under 200: tier 1. 200 to 1,000: tier 2. Over 1,000: tier 3.
2. **What software do you need the agent to integrate with?**
   Google Calendar plus one basic CRM: tier 1 works. Practice
   management systems, dispatch software, custom internal tools:
   tier 2 or 3.
3. **How much variability is there in your inbound calls?**
   Every call is basically a booking: tier 1 works. Multiple
   intents, multiple branches, judgment routing: tier 2 or 3.
4. **Do you have compliance requirements?**
   Nothing regulated: tier 1 works. HIPAA, TCPA-sensitive
   outbound, licensed-industry disclosure: tier 2 or 3.
5. **How much of the tuning work do you want to own personally?**
   All of it: tier 1. Some of it: tier 2. None of it: tier 3.

The trap most operators fall into is buying up a tier "just in
case." A dog groomer does not need a tier 3 deployment. A
regional plumbing franchise doing 4,000 calls per month should
not try to run on a tier 1 template.

## What to actually evaluate when comparing vendors

Once you have picked the tier, here are the things worth
comparing that most operators overlook.

### Voice quality on a real call

Do not evaluate voice quality from the demo on the vendor's
website. Those are cherry-picked. Ask for the vendor's demo
number and call it three times: once with a straightforward
booking request, once interrupting the agent mid-sentence, once
with a request the agent probably will not handle well. Listen
for:
- How natural the pacing is.
- Whether it handles interruption without falling apart.
- Whether it declines gracefully when it does not know.

Anything under a natural-sounding baseline in 2026 is a hard no.
Voice quality is a solved problem at the top of the market and
you should not be settling.

### Latency on the actual call

Response latency (time from you stopping speaking to the agent
starting to respond) is the single biggest factor in how "AI-like"
a call feels. Anything over 900 milliseconds feels slow. Anything
under 500 feels natural. Measure it on the demo call, do not take
vendor claims.

### Escalation logic

How does the agent decide to transfer? Test it. Ask to speak to
a human. Get frustrated. Ask a compound question. Watch how it
routes. Bad escalation is the number one CSAT killer in voice AI
deployments.

### Integration surface

Ask for a specific list of every integration the vendor supports
natively vs. via webhook vs. not at all. If your key integration
is "custom" or "coming soon," ask exactly what that means and how
long it takes.

### Recording, transcript, and QA workflow

Where do call recordings live? For how long? Who can access them?
How do you review and score AI calls? These questions matter more
after month three than they seem to matter at signup.

## The pricing traps to avoid

Three pricing patterns I see cause problems six months into a
deployment.

1. **Unlimited-minutes bundles.** Sounds great, works until you
   actually hit the "unlimited", vendors typically throttle
   quality on high-volume accounts.
2. **Per-conversation flat rate.** A "conversation" is a fuzzy
   billing unit. Ask exactly what counts and what does not.
3. **Setup fees under $500.** The setup work required to deploy
   a voice agent correctly does not fit into $500 of professional
   services. What you are getting is a templated bot that ships
   fast and gets scoped weakly.

The most transparent pricing model is a monthly platform fee
plus per-minute voice charges billed at cost or with a modest
markup. That model makes the ROI math easy to defend to yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I try a tier 1 solution first before committing to tier 2?**
If your operation legitimately fits tier 1, yes. If it does not
and you buy tier 1 anyway to save money, you will churn out in
90 days having built no institutional knowledge and having
frustrated the callers who tried it. Buy the tier that matches
the operation.

**Can I switch tiers later?**
Yes, and it is common. Most operators start at tier 2 and stay
there. A few graduate to tier 3 when they hit unique needs. Tier
1 buyers who outgrow it typically start over at tier 2 with a new
vendor.

**How do I evaluate the human team behind a tier 2 deployment?**
Ask three questions: how many deployments have you shipped in my
industry, what does your discovery process look like, and can I
speak with two of your current clients. Any tier 2 agency that
declines those questions is not serious.

**What about no-code voice agent builders?**
Fine for tier 1 use cases. Do not use them for anything you
actually depend on for revenue.

**Is voice AI going to replace all of this with something better
next year?**
Voice quality and latency improve every quarter. Deployment
patterns and integration surfaces are stable. What you build in
2026 will still work in 2028; you will just be running it on a
better voice model.

## Where a voice agent is the wrong buy at any tier

- Businesses where the owner personally answers every call and
  closes on it. That workflow works and does not need automation.
- Businesses doing under 50 inbound calls per month. Handle them
  yourself.
- Businesses in a moment of operational chaos (recent merger,
  active legal issue, staff turnover above 50 percent). Fix the
  underlying operation before layering automation on it.
