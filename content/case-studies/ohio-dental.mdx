## The before state

Ohio Dental runs two locations with five front-desk staff combined.
Average new-patient LTV is around **$1,820**, mix of routine cleanings,
restorative work, and a small cosmetic segment. The practice's
operational philosophy is in-house attention: the team is supposed to
own the patient experience from check-in through check-out.

The phone was breaking that philosophy. Carrier records from the 30
days before deployment:

- **Inbound calls/month:** 924 across both locations
- **Average duration:** 3m 17s
- **Front-desk hours spent on phone:** ~14 hrs/day combined
- **New-patient inquiries:** 142
- **New-patient calls answered live within 60 seconds:** 38%
- **Recorded no-show rate:** 14%

Hyder's framing during discovery: *"Our team spends daily 2-3 hours
answering the rings, and our in-house attention is vanishing."* The
practice was not losing patients to a competitor, they were losing
the quality of every in-chair interaction to the phone.

## What we shipped

A dental-tuned deployment with four work streams:

1. **New-patient intake script.** Insurance carrier + plan type, reason
   for visit, current dentist (yes/no), preferred location, contact
   info. Validates insurance against the in-network list before booking
   and surfaces out-of-network self-pay options when applicable.
2. **Live booking against Open Dental.** Books the specific provider
   per service type (hygienist for cleanings, doctor for restorative,
   specific operatory for surgical) honoring buffer time and provider
   block schedules.
3. **Two-touch confirmation sequence.** 48-hour SMS confirmation with
   a one-tap reschedule link, plus a 24-hour voice confirmation that
   the AI runs outbound. Calls flagged by the AI as "patient may
   reschedule" route into a same-day callback queue.
4. **Front-desk escalation rules.** Clinical questions, anyone asking
   for the doctor, billing disputes, and emergency-level pain calls
   route to a live front-desk team member with a one-line context
   briefing.

Live in 14 business days. Parallel-run for 5 days before cutover.

NPS scores that have moved from 71 to 84 over the deployment window.

Specific deltas:

- **New-patient calls answered live within 60 seconds:** 38% → 96%.
- **Median front-desk phone time per FTE per day:** 2h 40min → 24min.
- **No-show rate:** 14% → 9.6% (due to the two-touch confirmation).
- **Insurance-related re-asks at check-in:** down by roughly two-thirds
  because the AI captures insurance details during the booking call.

## What we learned

Dental has the highest capture rate in our portfolio, the call profile
is overwhelmingly booking, rescheduling, and insurance questions, which
the AI handles natively. The most material long-term effect, though,
is the in-chair NPS improvement. When the front desk is not under
phone pressure, the entire patient experience moves up a tier, and
that compounds into retention and referral over 12-18 months in a way
the booking number alone does not capture.
